📈 For Investors

What this business does, in plain English

  • What do you sell?

    We sell a physical AI computer called the Sentinel Node and a monthly hardware-as-a-service. The computer sits inside a doctor’s office or law firm and handles paperwork automatically — writing notes, drafting letters, summarizing documents — without ever connecting to the internet or a cloud server.

  • Who buys it?

    Independent medical practices with 2 to 10 physicians, and small law firms. These professionals handle sensitive records every day and face serious legal consequences if those records are exposed. They cannot afford the data-breach risk of cloud AI tools, but they also cannot keep up with the paperwork without help.

  • How do you make money?

    Customers pay a one-time setup fee of $5,000 and a monthly managed service subscription starting at $950. No per-use charges, no surprise invoices. Pocono AI deploys and manages the infrastructure — one monthly subscription covers hardware, software, maintenance, and support.

  • Why now?

    Federal regulators have issued six-figure fines for AI-related privacy violations. The average healthcare data breach now costs $670,000. At the same time, physicians report spending nearly six hours per day on paperwork. The market has a serious problem it cannot solve with cloud tools, and we are the only company offering a hardware-grade, zero-egress solution priced for small practices.

  • What risk remains?

    We are a pre-revenue company with early stage — in discussions with our first design-partner practice; timing TBD. No signed agreement yet. Sales cycles in healthcare can be long, and physician trust is earned slowly. Regulatory requirements may shift. We have one founder and a small team, so execution risk is real. These are honest risks and we believe they are manageable, not fatal.

🩹 For Doctors & Nurses

What changes in your day — and what doesn’t

  • What changes in your day?

    After a visit, you speak your notes or drop a recording into a folder on your computer. The Sentinel Node turns it into a finished clinical note, draft prior authorization, or referral letter. You review it, make any changes, and sign off. The step that used to take 30 to 45 minutes often takes under five.

  • What don’t you have to do anymore?

    You do not have to type out visit summaries, draft repetitive insurance letters from scratch, or stay late finishing charts. You do not have to trust a cloud service with your patients’ records. You do not have to sign a contract with a vendor that stores copies of your data on their servers.

  • What stays in your control?

    Everything. You review every draft before it goes anywhere. The system never submits anything automatically. Your patient files never leave your building — the hardware is in your office and there is no outbound network path. You remain the licensed professional responsible for every clinical decision.

  • Is this a replacement for your clinical judgment?

    No. It is a drafting tool, not a diagnostic tool. It writes paperwork based on what you tell it. You decide what is clinically correct. Think of it as a very fast, very private medical transcriptionist who never sleeps and never leaves the building.

  • What does it cost compared to a scribe?

    A remote medical scribe typically costs $1,500 to $2,500 per month per provider, and that service sends your patient audio to a third-party vendor. Our Phase I rate starts at $950 per month for the practice, and nothing leaves your office. See specs.html for full tier pricing.

⚖ For Lawyers

Privilege, data custody, and the audit trail

  • How is attorney-client privilege protected?

    Privilege depends on keeping communications confidential. When you send a client file to a cloud AI service, that data passes through servers owned by a third party — and most cloud terms allow the vendor to retain your data or use it for model training. The Sentinel Node processes everything locally inside your office, with no outbound connection. Privileged content never leaves the building, so there is no third party to waive privilege.

  • What data never leaves your office?

    All of it. Client names, case facts, document drafts, transcripts, and any file you run through the system stay on the dedicated infrastructure deployed in your office. The machine is not connected to any external server. After the Sentinel Node processes a file, it deletes the source from the watch folder. No copy is retained outside your network.

  • What audit trail do you get?

    Every action is logged locally: which file was processed, at what time, and what output was generated. This log lives on the same hardware under your control. You can produce it for a client, a regulator, or a court. It shows what the AI did, so your review is documentable as a deliberate professional act.

  • Does this create any new liability?

    The AI produces drafts, not final work product. You review and approve everything. The liability structure is the same as working with a paralegal or a junior associate — the licensed professional is responsible for what goes out the door. The local architecture actually reduces your exposure compared to cloud tools, because there is no vendor data-handling agreement to breach.

  • What about bar ethics rules?

    Most state bars have issued guidance requiring lawyers to take reasonable steps to protect client information when using AI. The Sentinel Node’s on-premises architecture is the most defensible position available: no third-party access, no cloud storage, no data transmission. We recommend reviewing your state bar’s specific guidance and consulting your malpractice carrier.

Frequently asked questions

Will this slow my computer?

No. The Sentinel Node is a separate, dedicated machine — a Dell Precision workstation that sits in your office. It does not run on your laptop or your front-desk computer. You interact with it through a simple folder on your local network, the same way you would use a shared drive. Your existing computers are not affected at all.

What if the internet goes down?

Nothing changes. The Sentinel Node does not use the internet. It runs entirely on your local network, with the AI models already installed on its hard drive. If your internet goes out, the Node keeps working. Internet access was never part of the architecture — that is a feature, not a limitation.

Who is liable if the AI makes a mistake?

You are, in the same way you are liable for any other work that goes out under your license. The AI produces a draft. You review it. You sign off on it. Nothing is submitted automatically. This is the same framework as working with a paralegal, a scribe, or a junior associate — the licensed professional takes responsibility for the final product. The audit log documents your review, which strengthens your position if your process is ever questioned.

How long does setup take?

Deployment typically takes one to three business days. We ship a pre-configured machine. Your IT person or our team connects it to your local network. Staff training is usually done in a single session. Most practices are running live work through the system within a week of receiving the hardware.

What happens to my data if I cancel?

Your data is within your locally deployed infrastructure in your office. Your data belongs to you. If you cancel, you receive a full data export before any infrastructure is retrieved. After the minimum 12-month term, the SSD drives containing your practice-specific indexed data are retained by your practice — your document repositories stay with you. There is nothing on a vendor server to delete, because nothing was ever sent there. You have full operational control of your data from day one.

Is this appropriate for use with patient data under HIPAA?

The architecture eliminates the main HIPAA risk pathway: transmission of protected health information to a third party. Because the system never sends data outside your office, there is no Business Associate Agreement required for the AI processing itself. That said, HIPAA compliance is a complete program, not a single vendor decision. We encourage you to involve your compliance officer in any deployment decision.
