Sentinel Node Deployment Realism

What Tuesday Morning Looks Like

Plain-language walkthrough of a Sentinel Node deployment — from the moment hardware arrives on-site, through document ingestion, staff training, governance setup, and the human-review procedures that keep professionals in control of every clinical and legal decision.

1
Day 0 — Before the box arrives

Preparation & site readiness

A short kickoff call confirms network topology, designated admin contacts, and the document sources you'll ingest first. We send a one-page checklist covering power, rack space (or shelf footprint), and a 1U ethernet drop.

  • Designate one clinical/legal admin and one IT contact
  • Identify first 3 document repositories to ingest (EHR exports, case files, policy library)
  • Confirm physical placement — locked closet, server room, or behind reception
2
Day 1 — Hardware on-site

Sentinel Node deployment

Hardware arrives pre-configured. On-site setup takes roughly 90 minutes: rack or shelf placement, network connection, and a hardware-custody form signed by your designated admin. The device boots into a local-only mode — no cloud handshake required to operate.

  • Physical placement + network connection
  • Hardware custody form signed (chain of physical control begins)
  • Local admin credentials issued — Pocono AI has no remote access by default
3
Day 1–3 — Knowledge base build

Document ingestion

The first document batches are ingested locally. Embeddings, the retrieval index, and the citation map all live on the Sentinel Node — nothing leaves the premises. Ingestion of a typical small-practice library (5,000–25,000 documents) completes within 24–48 hours.

  • OCR pass on scanned PDFs and image-based documents
  • Per-document access controls applied (role-based, matching your existing roles)
  • Citation pointers tied to source page numbers for audit traceability
4
Day 3–5 — Staff onboarding

Training & governance setup

A 60-minute training session for clinical or legal users covers the query interface, citation verification, and the human-review queue. A separate 45-minute session for admins covers role assignments, audit log access, and the governance dashboard.

  • End-user training: query syntax, citation verification, escalation
  • Admin training: roles, audit logs, governance dashboard
  • Written governance policy template provided — customize and adopt
5
Week 2–4 — Validation period

Human review & validation

The first month runs in mandatory human-review mode. Every AI-generated output passes through a licensed professional before it can be acted on. Disagreements are logged with reasons — that log becomes your governance artifact and the basis for any later workflow refinements.

  • 100% of outputs reviewed by a licensed clinician or attorney
  • Disagreement reasons captured in the audit log
  • Weekly check-in with Pocono AI to review patterns and tune retrieval
6
Ongoing — Steady state

Operations & support

After validation, the practice or firm sets its own review thresholds. Human-in-the-loop remains mandatory for any clinical or legal decision — that's a non-negotiable policy, not a configuration option. Pocono AI provides quarterly health checks and remote firmware support only on explicit, time-boxed request.

  • Local-only operation by default; no telemetry, no cloud sync
  • Quarterly on-site or remote health check
  • Audit log retained on-device, exportable to your existing systems

Human-in-the-loop is structural, not optional. Sentinel Node will not auto-execute clinical orders or file legal documents. A licensed professional verifies and acts on every output. That boundary is enforced at the application layer and surfaced in the audit log.

Day One: The First Real Workflow

The first successful Sentinel Node day is not measured by whether the model can answer a clever prompt. It is measured by whether staff can complete a bounded, cited, human-reviewed packet using approved local documents.

The first packet should be small in scope, clearly bounded, and fully reviewed before it is treated as anything more than a training exercise.

1

Confirm local source folder

Verify the approved pilot documents are ingested and in scope. Nothing runs until the approved source set is confirmed.

2

Generate one packet draft

Staff runs a bounded, single-workflow request using approved local documents. Scope is pre-defined — not open-ended.

3

Verify citations

Every claim in the draft is checked against its source. Staff flags any claim that cannot be confirmed in the cited page range.

4

Identify missing evidence

Staff documents any record or fact the system flagged as missing, stale, or unresolvable.

5

Route to reviewer

The draft — with citation notes and missing-evidence flags — goes to the Responsible Reviewer. Nothing is used before this step.

6

Capture reviewer corrections

The reviewer edits, accepts, or rejects. Every correction is logged as a pilot observation.

7

Log staff friction

Staff friction points — confusing prompts, unexpected outputs, missing sources — are recorded in the pilot friction log.

8

Do not use output until human-approved

No output is used professionally, filed, or transmitted until the Responsible Reviewer has approved it.

9

Debrief

15-minute debrief: what worked, what was missing, what the next workflow session should focus on.
