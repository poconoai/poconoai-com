Sentinel Node Clinical Deployment Framework

How the Sentinel Node lives inside your practice

A role-aware, audited deployment model for independent medical practices. Licensed providers, clinical support staff, and administrative staff each see a different slice of the same locally-indexed record — with mandatory human-in-the-loop review before any clinical or billing action.

Built for practices of 2–10 providers. No cloud calls. No vendor employees with access to your records. Every output cites a retrieved chunk from a document you own.

Who this deployment model is for

The Clinical Deployment Framework is designed for independent medical practices — family medicine, internal medicine, pediatrics, behavioral health, specialty solo and small group practices — that want AI assistance for documentation, prior authorization, chart prep, and care coordination without sending protected health information to a cloud vendor.

  • Practice size: 2 to 10 licensed providers, with associated nursing, MA, and administrative staff.
  • Technical posture: You have an EHR you can export documents from. You do not need a custom EHR integration to start.
  • Compliance posture: You are a covered entity. You take HIPAA seriously. You read the Notice of Privacy Practices you give patients and you mean it.
  • Time pressure: Your providers are spending 4–6 hours a day on EHR work (Sinsky et al., Annals of Family Medicine) and you have a prior-auth backlog that eats nursing time.

If you are a hospital system with 400+ beds and an enterprise Epic / Nuance DAX contract, this is not the right deployment for you. This page describes the practice-scale model.

Role-aware access — the three-tier model

The Sentinel Node is not one tool that everyone uses the same way. It is a role-aware AI deployment. The same locally-stored documents are accessible to all three tiers, but the query types, output formatting, and verification requirements are scoped to what each role is licensed and trained to do.

Tier A · Licensed Providers

Clinical Decision Support

Physicians, NPs, and PAs query the node for cited patient history before appointments, during care coordination, and for referral documentation. Every response cites the retrieved chunk from the locally-indexed record. The provider verifies before any clinical decision.

  • Pre-visit chart summaries with source citations
  • Differential support grounded in indexed records
  • Referral package generation
  • Discharge summary drafting
  • All outputs flagged "requires physician review"
Tier B · Clinical Support

Prep, Triage & Prior Auth

Medical assistants and nurses use the node to build complete prior authorization submissions from locally-indexed records, prep triage notes, and assemble medication histories. The aim is to reduce the wrongful denial rate by making sure submissions are complete on the first attempt.

  • Prior-auth packet assembly with linked source records
  • Medication history retrieval and verification
  • Intake triage documentation prep
  • Lab and imaging result context for nursing follow-up
  • Scope-limited to non-licensed clinical functions
Tier C · Administrative

Operations & Billing

Front-desk and billing staff query for policy context, code lookups, and patient communication history — without access to the full clinical record. Role-based query scoping keeps the right data in front of the right staff member.

  • Billing inquiry context retrieval
  • Insurance policy document queries
  • CPT and procedure code lookups
  • Patient communication history summaries
  • No access to provider clinical notes or differentials

Role-based access control (RBAC) is enforced at the API layer of the Sentinel Node, not just in the user interface. A billing clerk cannot construct a query that returns clinical notes, regardless of how the query is phrased.

Human-in-the-loop verification — the non-negotiable

The Sentinel Node does not make clinical or legal decisions. Every output is reviewed, edited, or approved by a licensed professional before it is used. This is not a marketing statement — it is enforced by the workflow.

How HITL is enforced

1. Every output cites a retrieved chunk. The clinician or staff member can see the source document inline alongside the generation. There is no "trust the model" path — the source is always visible.

2. Every output is flagged by tier. Tier A outputs are flagged "requires physician sign-off." Tier B outputs are flagged "requires licensed review before submission." Tier C outputs are flagged "for administrative use only — not clinical."

3. No autonomous action is taken. The node does not write to the EHR. It does not submit prior authorizations on its own. It does not send messages to patients. The output is staged for a human to review, edit, and act on.

4. The audit trail records the human step. Every query, retrieval, and human review action is recorded in the local audit log, retained 90 days locally, before archive per your data retention policy.

This is the architectural answer to the Mata v. Avianca hallucination problem in legal and the documented hallucination risk in clinical settings: don't trust generation alone — ground it in retrieval, cite the source, and require human verification.

Sample clinical workflows

Concrete examples of how the three tiers use the Sentinel Node day-to-day. Every workflow ends with a licensed professional reviewing and approving the output before action.

Prior authorization assembly (Tier B → Tier A)

  • Step 1Nurse queries: "Build a prior-auth packet for patient #12345, GLP-1 prescription, documenting prior failed therapies and current A1C."
  • Step 2Node retrieves chunks from the patient's record: A1C history, prior medication trial notes, current BMI documentation, prior cardiovascular workup.
  • Step 3Node assembles a draft packet citing each source chunk inline.
  • Step 4Nurse reviews, edits, attaches missing documents.
  • Step 5Physician signs off on clinical justification.
  • Step 6Audit log records: query, retrieved chunks, nurse review, physician sign-off, submission.

Pre-visit chart prep (Tier A)

  • Step 1Physician queries: "Summarize patient #67890's last three visits, focus on diabetes management and current med list."
  • Step 2Node retrieves the three most recent encounter notes and the active medication list from the locally-indexed record.
  • Step 3Node generates a 2–3 paragraph summary with inline citations to the source visits.
  • Step 4Physician reads, clicks through to a citation to verify a specific A1C trajectory, edits the summary, saves to prep notes.
  • Step 5Audit log records: query, retrieved sources, physician edit, time-on-task.

Referral package generation (Tier A)

  • Step 1Physician queries: "Generate a cardiology referral for patient #45678, include chest pain history, EKG findings, family history."
  • Step 2Node retrieves relevant cardiac history chunks, lab values, and imaging reports.
  • Step 3Node drafts a referral letter with cited findings and structured patient summary.
  • Step 4Physician reviews, edits the clinical question being asked of cardiology, signs.
  • Step 5Staff member faxes or transmits per practice protocol.

Patient communication context (Tier C)

  • Step 1Front desk receives patient call asking about a billing question.
  • Step 2Staff queries: "Show last three communications with patient #11223 and current outstanding balance — billing context only."
  • Step 3Node returns only communication history and billing data — no clinical notes (RBAC enforced at the API layer).
  • Step 4Staff resolves the inquiry with the context in hand.
  • Step 5Audit log records: query scope, retrieved data class, staff member identity.

These are illustrative workflows. Actual workflow configuration happens during the deployment scoping conversation — what your practice needs depends on your EHR, your staff structure, and your specialty.

Governance checkpoints

A clinical AI deployment is not "install and go." It is a governed system with explicit review points. The Pocono AI Clinical Deployment Framework includes these checkpoints by default:

  • Pre-deployment scopingPractice manager and lead physician sign off on which document classes are indexed, which roles get which tiers, and which workflows are enabled in the first 60 days. Documented in writing.
  • 30-day workflow reviewPractice reviews initial query patterns, output quality, time-on-task data, and any flagged outputs. Adjustments to RBAC scoping and workflow definitions made before broader rollout.
  • 60-day pilot reviewEnd of Proof-of-Value engagement: aggregate metrics reviewed (queries served, retrieval accuracy spot-checks, time reclaimed, errors caught at human-review stage). Decision point: convert to ongoing SLA or end engagement.
  • Quarterly audit log reviewPractice compliance officer or owner reviews the audit log: who queried what, what was generated, which outputs were flagged or rejected, any anomalous patterns. Required as a HIPAA technical safeguard.
  • Annual RBAC scope reviewStaff turnover, role changes, and workflow evolution mean the role-based access scope drifts. Annual review keeps the scoping accurate.
  • Incident response protocolIf the node produces an output that contributes to a clinical near-miss or error, the audit log provides the full retrieval chain. Root cause analysis is documented as part of the practice's existing clinical quality improvement process.

The audit trail

Every interaction with the Sentinel Node is recorded in a reviewable local audit log. This is a HIPAA technical safeguard requirement and a malpractice-defense asset. The log captures:

  • Query event: timestamp, authenticated user, role tier, query content, query scope (which document classes were searched).
  • Retrieval event: which document chunks were returned, their source records, confidence scores.
  • Generation event: the model output produced, with its citation links to the retrieved chunks.
  • Human review event: who reviewed, what edits were made, whether the output was approved, rejected, or modified.
  • Action event: if and when the reviewed output was used downstream (e.g., submitted to the EHR, sent to an insurer, attached to a referral).

Today, in the pilot, the audit log is append-only and reviewable: entries are written locally and are not modified or deleted in normal operation, and the system provides no in-product edit or delete path. The next audit-layer release (Stage 2) adds hash-chained entries — each entry including a cryptographic hash of the previous entry — so that tampering with historical entries breaks the chain and becomes cryptographically detectable. The log is retained locally for 90 days at high resolution, then archived per the practice's data retention policy. The log never leaves the practice. Pocono AI does not receive audit data.

Deployment timeline — from contract to operational

A typical clinical deployment runs about 60 days from signed engagement to operational, full role-aware workflow. The pace is set by the practice, not the vendor.

Week 1–2

Scoping & document inventory

Identify which document classes get indexed (clinical notes, labs, imaging reports, billing records). Define RBAC tiers and assign staff to each.

Week 2–3

Hardware delivery & network siting

Sentinel Node arrives. Practice IT (or our deployment partner) places it on a dedicated VLAN with no outbound internet route during inference.

Week 3–5

Indexing & pilot workflows

Initial document set indexed. Two or three priority workflows configured (typically prior-auth and chart-prep). First Tier A and Tier B users onboarded.

Week 5–8

Live operation & review

All tiers active. 30-day workflow review. Adjustments to scoping, prompts, and RBAC as needed before formal Proof-of-Value sign-off at day 60.

Engagement is structured as a Paid Proof-of-Value (POV). The POV fee covers scoping, deployment, and the 60-day pilot review. Conversion to ongoing SLA is the practice's decision at the end of the pilot.

Staff training & change management

Clinical AI fails when staff are not trained, not when the technology is wrong. The Pocono AI deployment includes role-specific training as a non-optional part of the 60-day engagement:

  • Tier A training (licensed providers): 90 minutes — how to query, how to read citation chains, when to override the output, how the audit log protects you.
  • Tier B training (clinical support staff): 60 minutes — prior-auth workflow walkthrough, when to escalate to a provider, scope boundaries.
  • Tier C training (administrative staff): 45 minutes — query scope, what they cannot see and why, how to handle out-of-scope questions.
  • Practice manager training: 60 minutes — audit log review, RBAC adjustment, monthly metrics interpretation.
  • Refresher cadence: 30-minute refresher at month 6 and annually thereafter.

Training is recorded for new staff onboarding. Practice retains the training materials — they are not licensed back from Pocono AI.
