Sentinel Node Local vs. Cloud

Why local AI infrastructure matters for regulated practices.

Cloud AI works well for many applications. It does not work well when data cannot leave the building, when the network goes down, when audit chains must be locally owned, or when a malpractice carrier asks what you shared with a third-party server. This page explains the structural differences — not as marketing, but as operational reality.

Scenario: the internet goes out at 9:00 AM.

Internet outages happen. For a cloud-dependent AI practice tool, the impact is immediate and complete. For a local deployment, the morning continues normally.

☁ Cloud AI practice — what happens
9:00 AM — Outage
Immediate halt
Every AI-assisted workflow stops. Prior auth tool, denial response drafting, chart review — all unavailable.
9:00 AM – recovery
Manual fallback
Staff reverts to manual document search and drafting. Efficiency gains disappear. Work queues back up.
Outage resolved
Partial recovery
Work restarts — but the backlog created during the outage creates cascading delays. Audit logs for the outage period may be incomplete.
🖥 Local AI practice (Sentinel Node) — what happens
9:00 AM — Outage
No impact
Sentinel Node does not contact any external server. Inference, retrieval, review, and audit logging continue without interruption.
9:00 AM – recovery
Normal operation
Staff complete their normal workflows. Approvals are logged locally. Audit envelopes are generated and stored on the appliance.
Outage resolved
Nothing to catch up
The practice has a complete, unbroken audit record for the entire period. The outage is operationally invisible to the AI workflow.
Why this matters beyond convenience: a broken audit chain during an outage period is not just an efficiency problem. In a regulated workflow, a gap in the review record during the period in question is a compliance exposure. Local infrastructure eliminates that exposure.

Latency: what "fast enough" means in a clinical workflow.

Raw inference speed matters less than total round-trip time in a real clinical or legal workflow. Cloud AI adds network latency on every query. Local inference does not.

Illustrative ranges based on typical API performance. Actual times vary by model, load, and network conditions. Local inference times are hardware-dependent.

Why latency accumulates

In a clinical workflow, AI is used iteratively — not once. A prior auth assembly may involve 6–12 retrieval queries as staff review and refine the evidence set. Each query adds latency. Local inference eliminates the network component from every single query.

Network variability vs. hardware variability

Cloud latency varies with internet conditions, API load, and rate limiting — all of which are outside the practice's control. Local inference variability is limited to hardware load, which the practice controls and can scale independently.

Four scenarios where local matters.

☁ Cloud AI — Scenario 1
The malpractice deposition question
A physician is deposed after an adverse outcome. The plaintiff's attorney asks: "Did you use AI in the preparation of this clinical document, and did that AI system transmit the patient's information to a third-party server?" The practice used a cloud AI tool. The answer is yes. The follow-up questions concern what was transmitted, when, and what the vendor's data retention policy was.
⚠ Cloud deployment creates a third-party data custody question in every deposition context.
🖥 Local AI — Scenario 1 response
The same deposition question
The physician answers: "Yes, we used a local AI system installed on our own hardware. The patient's data never left our network. Here is the audit record showing the query, the evidence retrieved from our own documents, the draft, and my approval." The audit envelope was generated locally and has been available since the day of the session.
✓ Local deployment produces a complete, locally-owned audit record that exists independent of any vendor.
☁ Cloud AI — Scenario 2
The privilege waiver question
A law firm uses a cloud AI tool to assist with case strategy analysis. A document containing privileged client communications is included in the query context. The communications are transmitted to the vendor's servers. Whether this constitutes a waiver of privilege — or creates a disclosure obligation — is a live question in several jurisdictions.
⚠ Cloud transmission of privileged material creates a privilege boundary question that varies by jurisdiction and has no settled answer.
🖥 Local AI — Scenario 2 response
Privilege stays inside the firm
The same analysis occurs on the firm's own hardware. The privileged document is retrieved from the firm's local corpus, processed by a local inference model, and the resulting draft is reviewed and approved within the firm's own systems. No privileged content ever reaches an external server.
✓ Local processing keeps privileged material within the firm's own custody at all times — the same boundary as any other internal work product.

When cloud AI is the right answer — and when it isn't.

Cloud AI is not wrong. It is the wrong architecture for specific use cases. Being clear about this distinction is part of how Pocono AI maintains credibility with technically sophisticated buyers.

Cloud AI works well when:

  • Data is not regulated or sensitive
  • No audit chain ownership is required
  • Internet availability can be assumed
  • General-purpose tasks (drafting, summarizing non-sensitive content)
  • Startup costs must be minimized and per-use pricing is acceptable
  • The organization has no compliance obligation around data residency

Local AI is the better answer when:

  • Patient, client, or privileged data is part of the workflow
  • The organization must own and produce its audit record
  • Operations must survive internet outages
  • Regulated output must not be finalized without human review attribution
  • The malpractice carrier or ethics board asks where data was processed
  • The practice cannot accept third-party data custody in any form
The honest framing: Pocono AI sells to the second category. If a practice is in the first category, cloud AI is probably fine and Sentinel Node is probably over-built for their needs. We are not interested in selling to buyers who don't need what we build.
