The audit lifecycle, from prompt to retention.

Every output the Sentinel Node produces passes through seven stages. Each stage records a audit entry into the append-only audit log. This page shows the mechanism — not as a marketing claim, but as the actual sequence of events your compliance officers, IT auditors, and defense counsel will be reviewing.

Seven stages, one append-only, reviewable log. The next audit-layer release (Stage 2) hash-chains each entry, so any retroactive tampering breaks the chain and is detectable. Stage 5 is human-required; the others are automatic.

The seven stages, in detail

Each stage below corresponds to a numbered box in the diagram above. The blue field strings show the actual log entry structure for that stage — see the audit log worked example for what a sequence of entries looks like together.

1
milliseconds

Prompt — the user's query is hashed and recorded.

When a clinician or attorney submits a prompt, the Sentinel Node computes a SHA-256 hash of the query text and emits an audit entry recording who issued it, when, and from which workstation. The raw query is also stored, but the hash is what chains forward.

event: "query" · user · role · prompt_hash · ts
2
~200 ms

Retrieval — the relevant document chunks are pulled.

Hybrid retrieval (dense vector similarity + sparse keyword + reranker) identifies the document chunks most likely to ground the response. Every chunk retrieved is recorded by document ID and page range. If the user's role does not permit access to a candidate document, it is excluded before reranking — and that exclusion is itself logged.

event: "retrieval" · doc_ids · page_ranges · embed_scores · access_denials
3
1–4 s

Response — the LLM drafts a grounded response.

The retrieved chunks are passed as context to the local language model, which drafts a response and emits inline citations referencing the source chunks. The response text and its SHA-256 hash are recorded; the response is never finalized at this stage — it's a draft awaiting verification.

event: "response" · response_hash · citation_count · token_count
4
~100 ms

Citation verification — claims are checked against sources.

Before the response reaches a human reviewer, each cited claim is programmatically checked against the actual content of its cited page range. Mismatches — where a claim is not located in the cited source — are flagged. This is the system's first line of defense against hallucinated citations and is the most common trigger for rejection at the next stage.

event: "verify" · per-citation status · mismatch_count · low_confidence_flags
5
human-required

Human review — a licensed professional makes the call.

The cited draft, with all citations and review flags visible, is presented to a licensed clinician or attorney for review. The reviewer chooses one of three dispositions: accept as-is, accept with edit, or reject with reason. Reject reasons are categorical (citation_mismatch, out_of_scope, policy_violation, etc.) and free-text notes are also captured. This stage cannot be bypassed via configuration — it is enforced at the application layer and visible in the audit log on every output.

event: "review" · reviewer · decision · reason · edit_distance
6
finalize

Audit log append — entries are written to a reviewable log today; chaining and signing are Stage 2.

All entries from the preceding stages are committed to the reviewable, append-only audit log. The next audit-layer release (Stage 2) gives each entry the previous entry's chain_hash as its prev_hash field, so tampering with any entry breaks the chain from that point forward and is detectable in O(n) time by recomputing hashes. In Stage 2 the day's entries are also signed with the Sentinel Node's attestation key, providing cryptographic evidence of provenance.

today: reviewable append-only log · Stage 2: prev_hash · chain_hash · daily attestation
7
90 days min.

Retention — logs are retained on-device, exportable on demand.

Audit logs are retained on the Sentinel Node for a minimum of 90 days (configurable to longer retention periods to match your governance policy). Logs can be exported in JSONL or CSV format for ingestion into your SIEM, compliance system, or e-discovery platform. The retention period and export format are part of the adopted governance policy; Pocono AI does not unilaterally alter retention behavior.

on-device storage · JSONL/CSV export · policy-governed

What this gives you

The seven-stage lifecycle isn't audit theater. It's a set of specific verification properties that compliance officers, regulators, and opposing counsel can independently check.

⧖

Provenance

Every output traces back to the specific user, role, prompt, and retrieved source chunks. No anonymous AI outputs.

≡

Traceability

Every cited claim is linked to a specific document and page range. Reviewers verify against source, not against the model's parametric memory.

#

Tamper-evidence

SHA-256 chaining means any retroactive edit breaks the chain. Detection is mathematical, not procedural.

✓

Human accountability

Every AI output has a named reviewer, a recorded decision, and a reason if rejected. Liability has a paper trail.

⌖

Data sovereignty

The entire lifecycle executes on-premises. No prompt or document leaves the device during normal operation.

⊟

Exportability

Logs export in standard formats. You own the audit trail; it isn't held hostage in a vendor system.
