Sentinel Node Attorney-Client Privilege

How privilege is preserved when AI is in the workflow.

Privilege is the gating question. Every legal-AI conversation either answers it credibly in the first five minutes or it ends. This page is the answer — four technical and operational mechanisms that keep privileged material privileged, and an explicit statement of what the Sentinel Node will not do.

Four mechanisms

Privilege preservation is not a policy claim. It is the joint operation of four architectural and procedural constraints. Removing any one weakens the others.

1
On-premises retrieval

Privileged material never leaves the premises.

The Sentinel Node performs all retrieval, embedding, and inference on hardware physically located in the firm's office. There is no cloud handshake, no remote API call, no model-provider visibility into queries or documents. Outbound WAN traffic is structurally blocked at the network configuration layer and verified daily via signed egress attestation.

  • Zero bytes outbound to external services during normal operation
  • No model-provider exposure — embeddings and inference both local
  • Daily signed attestation; tampering is detectable in the audit chain
2
Role-based document access

Privilege follows the document, not the user's whim.

Every document ingested into the Sentinel Node carries explicit access controls. A document marked privileged is retrievable only by users whose role includes that privilege scope. Junior associates, paralegals, support staff, and contract reviewers see the documents their role permits — and only those. The same access matrix that governs your case-management or document-management system can be imported at ingest time.

  • Per-document role grants set at ingest, modifiable on demand
  • Role changes apply immediately; revoked roles cannot retrieve previously-accessible documents
  • Access denials are logged with reason, supporting forensic review
3
Reviewable audit trail

Every retrieval is recorded and reviewable today.

Every prompt, every retrieved document chunk, every AI-drafted response, and every attorney-review decision is appended to a reviewable audit log. Each entry references the previous entry's hash, so any retroactive edit breaks the chain and is immediately detectable. If a privileged document was retrieved, the audit log records who retrieved it, when, in response to what query, and what was done with the result.

  • Per-retrieval record: user, role, prompt hash, document IDs, timestamp
  • Per-response record: response hash, citations, reviewer, decision
  • Chain integrity is verifiable independently of the device
4
Human-in-the-loop, structurally

An AI draft is never a filing.

The Sentinel Node produces drafts. It does not file documents, send communications, or take action that could waive privilege. Every AI-generated output passes through a licensed attorney's review before any external communication or filing. This is enforced at the application layer and visible in the audit log — there is no configuration option that bypasses it.

  • 100% review coverage during the validation period (typically the first month)
  • Disagreements are logged with reason; rejection reasons aggregate weekly
  • No auto-execution path exists for filings, communications, or settlement actions

What this means in practice

The four mechanisms together produce specific operational guarantees. These are commitments — they are also things the system structurally does not do.

The Sentinel Node will…

  • Retrieve privileged material only for authorized users
  • Record every retrieval in an append-only, reviewable log
  • Produce drafts grounded in cited source documents with page-level pointers
  • Surface low-confidence retrievals and citation mismatches to the reviewer
  • Provide a daily egress attestation showing zero WAN outbound
  • Export the audit log in formats your SIEM or compliance system can ingest
  • Revoke a role's access without rebuilding the document index

The Sentinel Node will not…

  • Transmit privileged content to a third-party model provider
  • Auto-file, auto-send, or auto-execute any communication
  • Permit retrieval bypass for any user, including system administrators
  • Disable human-in-the-loop review via configuration
  • Retain logs in a form that conceals chain-of-custody
  • Operate without a signed governance policy in place
  • Replace the attorney's professional judgment on privilege classification

Where this fits with the rules of professional conduct

Pocono AI is not your ethics counsel. The Sentinel Node provides the technical scaffolding that supports compliance with the rules; the firm's adopted governance policy is what makes that compliance binding. Three rules sit closest to the AI question; the malpractice liability analysis treats them in more depth.

  • ABA Model Rule 1.1 (Competence). Comment 8 (technology competence) requires lawyers to keep abreast of changes in the law and its practice, including the benefits and risks of relevant technology. Local retrieval-augmented drafting falls inside that perimeter.
  • ABA Model Rule 1.6 (Confidentiality). A lawyer must make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized disclosure of client information. On-premises retrieval with no WAN egress is a reasonable-effort standard that exceeds most cloud-AI alternatives.
  • ABA Model Rule 5.3 (Supervision of nonlawyer assistance). The lawyer remains responsible for the conduct of nonlawyer assistance. The Sentinel Node is non-lawyer assistance. Human-in-the-loop review is the supervision.

References & further reading

  • ABA Formal Opinion 512 — Generative AI Tools, July 2024. The first ABA guidance on lawyers' use of generative AI; treats Rule 1.1, 1.6, 5.3, and 5.5 explicitly.
  • Mata v. Avianca, 22-cv-1461 (S.D.N.Y. 2023) — sanctions for AI-fabricated citations. The most-cited precedent for why generative AI must not be the final author of a filing.
  • Wadsworth v. Walmart — Fifth Circuit AI-citation sanctions; affirmed at appellate level. Confirms that the originating attorney remains responsible for AI-produced citations.
  • The Paradox of Automated Abandonment — Pocono AI's full malpractice-liability analysis. Four cases, three jurisdictions, three liability theories.
  • The Hidden Cost of Fragmented Legal Information — research brief on retrieval fatigue, ABA Formal Opinion 512, and the case for governed retrieval over autonomous lawyering.
