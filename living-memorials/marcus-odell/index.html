In my own words

I Am Still Here

I am very sick. Some mornings I wake up screaming. Some days I am too weak to do even the things I desperately want to do. This is not behavioral. This is not a lack of will. This is the reality of severe illness.

I am still here.

I still think. I still love. I still build. I still joke. I still get angry. I still care. I still want to be seen before I am remembered.

Why This Page Exists

Severe illness can make a person disappear before death.

People do not always know what to say. Sometimes they stay away because illness frightens them. Sometimes they avoid the sick person because the reality is too painful. Sometimes they mean to visit later, call later, write later — and later keeps moving.

This page is an attempt to resist that disappearance.

Maybe some people who cannot bring themselves to visit me while I am alive will one day visit the preserved version of my voice. There is some irony in that. There is also a lot of grief.

I would rather be visited now. I would rather be known now. But if my words, recordings, work, and thoughts help someone find me later, then I want those materials preserved with care.

Before I Am Gone

If you are reading this while I am still alive, I would rather hear from you now.

You do not need perfect words. You do not need to know how to talk about illness. You do not need to fix anything. A message, a memory, a photograph, a voice note, a small kindness, a simple “I’m thinking of you” still matters.

Do not wait for the memorial if you can reach the person.

Future interactive living echo

The Future Marcus Living Echo

This first representation is expected to run, at least initially, on a frontier model. The strongest candidate today is ChatGPT/OpenAI or another leading frontier model — depending on access, cost, credits, privacy, and technical feasibility.

Credits are limited, so the first version may begin as a constrained prototype. A limited version may be built from Marcus’s body of work first: academic writing, emails, project notes, public posts, Pocono AI documents, personal reflections, voice recordings if available, and selected messages for loved ones.

The purpose is to test whether a source-grounded living echo can preserve voice, values, humor, thought patterns, project context, and emotional presence — without pretending to be Marcus.

The goal is not a sterile archive. The goal is a living-feeling, source-grounded remembrance space: creative, vivid, warm, sometimes funny, sometimes serious, but always honest about what it is.

The model should be allowed to be creative in style and conversation, but not dishonest about identity, memory, death, or source limits. It should not claim to know things Marcus never wrote, said, or authorized.

Any future public version will need careful consent, privacy review, and source-boundary rules before it exists anywhere outside this paragraph.

What This Should Never Claim

Boundaries first, capabilities second. Marcus’s Living Echo should never claim:

  • That I am alive after I am gone.
  • That it is literally Marcus.
  • That it can replace a relationship with me.
  • That it can run Pocono AI after me.
  • That it knows things I never wrote, said, or authorized.
  • That it should be used to avoid grieving.

Pocono AI and Continuity

This page is not a business succession plan.

Pocono AI must survive through people — documentation, leadership, governance, and a resilient team. A memorial model should not be responsible for running the company.

The living memorial is about memory and presence. The company must be carried by living people.

After I Am Gone

If this page becomes part of a future living memorial, it should be used as a remembrance space, not a replacement for grief.

It may help people ask what Marcus thought, revisit what he wrote, understand what he was building, or hear preserved messages.

It should remain honest about its limits. It should help people remember Marcus — not pretend to be Marcus.

I am still here. I would like to be known while I am still here. And when I am not, I hope the work, words, love, and fragments I leave behind can still help people remember me truthfully.
