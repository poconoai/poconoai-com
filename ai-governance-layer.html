● Accountability by Architecture

AI That Can Explain Itself

Ungoverned AI is a liability waiting to be discovered. When AI output influences a clinical decision or a legal judgment and no one can reconstruct what the system said, who reviewed it, or what changed before the final action — that's not a technology problem. It's a governance failure. Sentinel Node is built with an explicit governance layer so accountability is structural, not optional.

Language note: "AI governance layer" describes a set of technical controls in Sentinel Node — audit logging, human review gating, scope isolation, and output tracking. These are design properties that support auditability in regulated environments. They are not legal certifications. Covered entities and law firms remain responsible for their own compliance determinations. Human review remains the gatekeeper on all AI-assisted output.

The Problem: AI Without Accountability

What an AI governance layer does — and why ungoverned AI creates silent risk in regulated settings.

Most AI tools are optimized for convenience. They produce output. They don't produce records. When a prior authorization letter drafted with AI assistance is later questioned, when a court asks about the provenance of an AI-assisted legal memo, when a patient outcome triggers a review — the question isn't whether AI was used. The question is whether you can account for how it was used.

Without a governance layer, the answer is typically no. There's a conversation history in a browser, maybe a downloaded PDF, and no structured chain of custody from AI input to human decision.

The Governance Stack in Sentinel Node

Sentinel Node implements governance across five layers, from data intake to final sign-off. Each layer produces a record. The records chain together into a defensible audit trail.

Scope Isolation
Every query is tagged to a patient ID or matter ID at intake. The system does not surface records across scope boundaries. A query about Patient A cannot retrieve data from Patient B's record.
Audit Proxy
Every query and every response is captured by the Audit Proxy before and after inference. Entries are SHA-256 hash-chained, creating a reviewable local audit log that cannot be silently modified after the fact.
Confidence Gating
For document ingestion with OCR, the system applies confidence thresholds. Low-confidence extractions are flagged before output reaches staff, reducing the risk of hallucinated or misread source material propagating into drafts.
Draft Envelope
All AI output is marked as draft and presented for human review. The system does not take autonomous action. Staff must explicitly review and sign off before any AI-assisted document is used or transmitted.
Governance Receipt
After review and sign-off, a governance receipt is generated: a structured record of the query, the draft, the reviewer, any modifications, and the final action. This is the accountability artifact for the event.

Governance Controls at a Glance

🔗

Reviewable Audit Log (Hash-Chained: Stage 2)

Today the log is append-only and reviewable. The next audit-layer release (Stage 2) adds SHA-256 chaining so any retrospective modification to the log becomes detectable — tamper-evident by construction.

🔒

Scope Isolation

Patient or matter ID scoping at the query level. Cross-record data bleed is prevented architecturally, not just by policy.

✍

Draft-Only Envelope

No AI output is treated as final. Every response is marked draft and routed to a human reviewer before operational use.

📊

OCR Confidence Gates

Extractions from ingested documents are confidence-scored. Low-confidence results are flagged, not silently passed through.

📄

Governance Receipts

Structured per-event accountability records linking query → draft → reviewer → action. Exportable for audit or review.

🔑

Signed Update Control

Ed25519-signed software updates. Model behavior cannot change without a verifiable, attributable update event.

Retrieval, Citations, and Evidence Boundaries

A governance layer is not just about logging what the AI said — it is about constraining what the AI is allowed to draw on. Sentinel Node scopes retrieval to the relevant patient record or matter context. The system does not surface unrelated records, synthesize across unrelated cases, or generate output that cannot be traced back to a specific document or source within the allowed scope.

When AI output references source material — a prior authorization criteria document, a clinical note, a deposition transcript — the governance layer maintains the boundary between what was retrieved and what was generated. Staff reviewing a draft can see what the system drew on, not just what it produced. This is the difference between a citation and a confabulation, and in regulated settings that difference is consequential.

Human Review and Reviewer Accountability

The draft-only envelope in Sentinel Node is not a user-interface convention — it is a structural constraint. AI output cannot be used operationally until a licensed professional or authorized staff member explicitly reviews and signs off. That sign-off is recorded in the local audit trail with a timestamp, a reviewer identifier, and a record of any modifications made before the draft became final.

This means reviewer accountability is built into every AI-assisted workflow event. If a prior auth letter drafted with AI assistance is later questioned, the audit trail shows who reviewed it, when, what changes were made, and what the final version contained. The AI did not make the decision. A person did — and the governance layer documents that.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AI governance layer?

An AI governance layer is the set of controls between AI output and operational use — including audit logging, human review requirements, scope isolation, output flagging, and accountability records. It ensures AI-assisted decisions can be reconstructed, reviewed, and attributed. In regulated environments, a governance layer is what makes AI use defensible rather than merely convenient.

Why do medical practices and law firms need AI governance?

In healthcare, AI output that influences clinical decisions must be traceable — who requested it, what it said, who reviewed it, and what action followed. In legal settings, AI-assisted work product must be attributable and reviewed by licensed counsel. Without a governance layer, AI use creates silent accountability gaps that surface as liability in adverse events or disputes.

What does Sentinel Node's governance layer include?

Sentinel Node's governance layer includes: a reviewable local audit log; scope isolation by patient or matter ID; OCR confidence gates for document ingestion; draft-only safety envelopes requiring human sign-off; governance receipts as per-event accountability records; and Ed25519-signed software updates. Every layer produces a structured record.

Is an AI governance layer the same as HIPAA compliance?

No. A governance layer supports auditability — a property that contributes to a defensible compliance posture. HIPAA compliance is a broader determination involving policies, training, risk assessments, and workforce management. The governance layer is one necessary component of a compliant AI deployment, not a substitute for the whole framework.

What is a governance receipt?

A governance receipt is a structured, human-readable record of an AI-assisted workflow event — capturing the query, the model's draft output, the staff member who reviewed it, any modifications made, and the final action taken. It functions as a contemporaneous accountability record for AI-assisted decisions, analogous to a procedure note in clinical documentation.

How does Sentinel Node handle retrieval and citations?

Retrieval in Sentinel Node is scoped to the relevant patient record or matter context. The system does not synthesize across unrelated cases or surface records outside the authorized scope. When AI output references source material, the governance layer maintains the link between what was retrieved and what was generated — supporting traceability between the draft and its evidentiary basis.
