Sentinel Node Infrastructure Philosophy

Local-first inference, with explicit governed handoff.

Most enterprise AI conversations treat the architecture as a binary — either everything runs in the cloud, or everything runs on-premises. That framing fails the regulated practice. The right architecture is hybrid by design and governed by policy: privileged and PHI-bearing work stays local; the rest can be policy-routed to a cloud provider when that's the right trade-off. The Sentinel Node is built around this distinction.

Every request enters the policy router. Privileged or PHI-bearing work flows to the local Sentinel Node. Non-sensitive work may optionally flow to a cloud LLM, with the handoff itself recorded in the audit log. Cloud handoff is disabled by default; enabling it is a deliberate, policy-governed decision.

What goes where, and why

The dividing line is not "is AI involved." The dividing line is "could this content waive privilege, violate HIPAA, or otherwise damage the practice if it left the building." Local-first is the default; cloud handoff is a deliberate exception that the practice opts into per policy, per query class, or not at all.

Always Local

The Sentinel Node handles this — period.

  • Patient charts, EHR exports, lab results, imaging reports
  • Privileged communications, attorney work product, case files
  • Prior-auth drafts referencing specific patients
  • Legal-research drafts referencing privileged matter
  • Any query that retrieves from the firm's or practice's document corpus
  • Any output destined for a clinical or legal filing
Eligible for Handoff (opt-in)

If the practice's policy permits, these may route to a cloud LLM.

  • General-knowledge queries with no document retrieval
  • Boilerplate drafting (form letters, generic policies, training material)
  • Writing-assistance tasks on already-public content
  • Translation or summarization of public-domain material
  • None of the above is enabled by default; each requires an explicit policy entry naming the query class and the provider

The four principles

Why "hybrid by design and governed by policy" is the right framing for regulated practice. These principles are what the architecture encodes.

1

Local-first is the default

If there's no explicit policy entry routing a request elsewhere, it goes to the local Sentinel Node. There is no quiet fallback.

2

Handoff is policy-bounded

Cloud handoff requires a written policy entry naming the query class, the provider, and the conditions. The policy is signed by the practice principal.

3

Every handoff is logged

If a request is routed to a cloud LLM, the routing decision itself is recorded in the append-only audit log — including which policy entry authorized it.

4

Content classification is conservative

The policy router defaults to "treat as sensitive" when classification is ambiguous. Borderline content stays local. False positives cost time; false negatives cost privilege.

Why this isn't just "private cloud"

A common cloud-AI counter-argument is: "our cloud is private, encrypted, and BAA-covered, so it satisfies your sovereignty concerns." That framing collapses two distinct properties. Hybrid orchestration treats them separately.

  • Encryption-at-transit and at-rest is necessary but not sufficient. It protects against an interceptor, not against the model provider itself, not against subpoena, and not against a future breach of the provider's infrastructure.
  • A Business Associate Agreement creates contractual liability, but the data has already left the practice. The compliance argument is paper, not architectural.
  • Local-first inference resolves both. The content never leaves the premises in the first place. The handoff for non-sensitive work happens with deliberate, audited consent — not as the default behavior.

The malpractice and regulatory exposure of cloud-AI hallucinations is the subject of our malpractice-liability analysis in more depth. The infrastructure-fragmentation argument — why local-first is a 2026 enterprise pattern, not a niche concern — is covered in our research hub.
