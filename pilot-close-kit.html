What the pilot proves

Four questions a 60-day pilot answers.

A Sentinel Node pilot is not a sales demo. It is a bounded proof-of-value inside your own network with your own documents and your own reviewer. At day 60, you have a written evidence report you generated yourself.

Draft quality

Can the system produce source-cited first drafts of your specific document type — prior authorizations, discovery summaries, intake letters — at a quality a reviewer would accept with minor edits?

Reviewer time savings

Does the assisted workflow measurably reduce the time your reviewer spends per document? The pilot measures baseline versus assisted and gives you a documented delta.

Audit trail integrity

Does every AI-assisted output carry an immutable log entry with timestamp, source citations, and reviewer disposition — accept, edit, or reject? Can your compliance officer inspect it?

Data sovereignty

Did your documents leave the building? Zero-egress deployment means network logs can confirm that sensitive records stayed on your premises. The pilot generates that confirmation.

What is included

The full 60-day package.

A Sentinel Node pilot is a managed deployment, not a software download. Pocono AI handles the infrastructure. Your practice provides one workflow and one named reviewer.

Hardware appliance

A Sentinel Node appliance ships to your location, preconfigured for local-only inference. No cloud routing required for standard workflows. Hardware remains Pocono AI property during the pilot.

RAG pipeline for one workflow

Retrieval-Augmented Generation configured for your document type: prior authorization templates, discovery review, intake letters, or similar. Source citations are embedded in every draft.

HITL reviewer interface

A browser-based review queue where your named reviewer accepts, edits, or rejects every AI-assisted draft. No output leaves the queue without a human disposition recorded.

Reviewable audit log (tamper-evident: Stage 2)

Every document event — draft generated, reviewed, edited, signed — is written to an append-only, reviewable local log. Exportable as a CSV or PDF at any time.

60-day evidence report

A written report at day 60 documenting baseline time, assisted time, draft acceptance rate, rejection reasons, missing-source prevention events, and reviewer trust score.

Deployment and technical support

Remote installation support, VLAN configuration guidance, and a direct line to the Pocono AI technical team throughout the 60-day window. No ticket queues.

Buyer checklist

What you need to have ready before the conversation.

This checklist is not a barrier. It is how we scope a pilot that will produce useful evidence rather than inconclusive noise. If you cannot yet answer an item, we can discuss it during the initial conversation.

  • One named document workflow. You have identified a specific document type your practice produces regularly — prior authorizations, referral letters, discovery summaries, intake assessments, or similar. Not a general "we want AI for everything."
  • One named reviewer. You have a specific licensed professional — physician, attorney, or delegated clinical staff — who will review AI-assisted drafts during the 60 days. They do not need to be the only user; they need to be the named reviewer for audit purposes.
  • A volume estimate. You have a rough sense of how many documents this workflow produces per week. Even a range helps scope whether the pilot will generate statistically meaningful evidence by day 60.
  • A privacy and compliance authority. You have identified who in your organization will assess the privacy posture of the deployment — compliance officer, privacy counsel, or practice administrator with this responsibility. They do not need to participate in the pilot, but they should be briefed before deployment.
  • A basic IT contact. You have someone who can confirm network topology, assist with VLAN configuration, and verify that the appliance does not route through cloud services during the pilot. This can be an outsourced MSP.
  • Decision clarity. You have a working definition of what a successful pilot looks like for your practice — what time savings, draft quality, or audit documentation would lead to a Phase I managed service conversation.
IT checklist

What your IT team or MSP needs to confirm.

The Sentinel Node is a physical appliance with an Ethernet connection and a browser-based interface. It does not require cloud accounts, vendor portals, or SaaS credentials. These items are the full IT surface area of the pilot.

  • Available Ethernet port on your LAN. Gigabit preferred; 100 Mbps minimum. The appliance connects to your local network only.
  • Static or DHCP-reserved IP within your VLAN. The reviewer browser interface requires a stable local address. We recommend a DHCP reservation at your router or managed switch.
  • Outbound HTTPS (port 443) confirmed not required. Confirm with your firewall admin that the appliance can be placed on a VLAN without outbound internet access for AI inference. (Firmware updates may require a brief, supervised internet window.)
  • No proxy or SSL-inspection for the appliance subnet. Deep-packet inspection on the appliance subnet can interfere with the local API. Confirm your firewall handles this subnet as trusted internal traffic.
  • Reviewer browser confirmed: Chrome, Firefox, or Edge. The review interface is a standard web app. Mobile browsers are supported for review; upload functions require desktop.
  • Documents available in PDF or plain text format. The RAG pipeline ingests PDF, DOCX, and TXT. EHR export to PDF is the most common path for clinical practices. No EHR integration is required for the pilot.
  • Power and physical space. The appliance requires a standard outlet and 2U of rack space or a flat surface in a secured equipment room. Dimensions and power draw provided on request.
  • Return shipping access. At pilot conclusion, the hardware must be returnable via standard carrier. Pocono AI provides a prepaid label if the pilot does not convert.
Reviewer responsibilities

What the named reviewer does during 60 days.

The reviewer is the most important person in the pilot. The quality of the evidence report is directly proportional to the consistency and honesty of reviewer dispositions. A reviewer who marks everything "accepted" produces a useless audit log.

  • Review each AI-assisted draft in the queue before it is used. Drafts should not be submitted to payers, courts, or clients without a human review and signoff in the interface.
  • Record honest dispositions. Accept with no edits, accept with minor edits (note what changed), accept with major edits (note what changed), or reject with a reason. This is the primary data the evidence report is built from.
  • Flag missing or incorrect source citations. If the draft cites a source that does not exist in the uploaded document set, or misrepresents a source, reject the draft and note the source error. These events are tracked as "missing-source prevention events."
  • Note time per document. At baseline and during the pilot, record roughly how long a document of this type takes to produce from scratch versus with AI assistance. Approximate ranges are sufficient; surgical precision is not required.
  • Participate in a mid-pilot check-in (approximately day 30) to discuss early findings and calibrate any workflow adjustments with the Pocono AI team.
  • Review the draft evidence report at day 55–58 before it is finalized. The reviewer should confirm that the documented metrics accurately reflect their experience of the workflow over 60 days.

What is measured over 60 days

Eight data points the evidence report documents.

Every metric is collected from the reviewable audit log and reviewer dispositions. No metrics are self-reported by Pocono AI. The practice can export and review the underlying log data independently.

Metric What it measures How it is collected
Baseline time per document Time to produce one document of this type without AI assistance Reviewer self-report at pilot start; logged by workflow type
Assisted time per document Time to review and approve an AI-assisted draft of the same document type Queue entry and reviewer disposition timestamp delta
Draft acceptance rate — minor edits Percentage of drafts accepted with only minor corrections Reviewer disposition log: “accepted with minor edits”
Draft acceptance rate — major edits Percentage of drafts requiring substantial revision before use Reviewer disposition log: “accepted with major edits”
Draft rejection rate Percentage of drafts rejected by the reviewer Reviewer disposition log: “rejected” with reason field
Missing-source prevention events Drafts caught containing missing or incorrect source citations before use Reviewer disposition log: rejection reason “source error”
Reviewer confidence score Reviewer’s self-assessed trust in the system at day 30 and day 60 Structured check-in survey (1–5 scale); logged and reported
Audit completeness Percentage of document events with complete reviewable audit-log entries Automated audit-log integrity check run at day 60

What happens after the pilot

Three options at day 60.

01

Convert to Phase I

The evidence report supports conversion. The 24-month managed service agreement activates. The pilot hardware remains in place or is replaced with production-tier hardware. Phase I pricing is locked for the first 12 months.

02

Extend the pilot

The evidence is promising but incomplete — perhaps document volume was lower than expected, or the reviewer wants a second workflow tested. An extension can be scoped and priced separately.

03

Return the hardware

The pilot does not produce evidence that supports conversion for this practice at this time. Hardware is returned via prepaid carrier. The $5,000 implementation fee is non-refundable. The full data export is yours to keep.

04

You keep the evidence

Regardless of outcome, the 60-day evidence report, the exported audit log, and all indexed documents belong to the practice. This is your data, and the record of what the system did with it is yours to inspect.
