Sentinel Node Pilot Readiness Kit

Know whether a Sentinel Node pilot is ready to succeed.

A strong pilot is not "install AI and see what happens." It is a scoped proof-of-value with named workflows, known document sources, review gates, defined success criteria, and a support cadence. This kit gives you everything needed to assess readiness, structure the engagement, and measure what happened at the end of 60 days.

Pre-pilot readiness checklist.

Complete this checklist before scheduling an installation. Items marked Required must be resolved before hardware is deployed. Recommended items improve pilot quality significantly. Optional items can be addressed during the pilot period.

Required — must be resolved before deployment
Recommended — significantly improves pilot quality
Optional — can be addressed during pilot period

Workflow Scope

✓

Primary workflow named

One or two specific workflows are identified before deployment: prior authorization support, denial response, policy retrieval, matter-file review, intake support, or compliance documentation.

Why it matters: scope creep is the most common pilot failure mode. A named workflow creates a measurable success condition.
✓

Task frequency estimated

How many times per week does this workflow occur? This determines baseline measurement and expected time-savings calculation.

Target: at least 5–10 tasks per week to generate meaningful operating evidence during the 60-day period.
○

Current time-per-task measured

Staff records actual time spent on the chosen workflow for 2 weeks before deployment. Creates the baseline against which improvement is measured.

Without a pre-pilot baseline, the "before" comparison is subjective. Two weeks of timed tracking is sufficient.

Document Corpus

✓

Initial corpus inventory completed

A list of document folders, file types, and sources that will be indexed is agreed before deployment. This includes payer policy documents, chart export formats, practice protocols, matter file locations, and template libraries.

The corpus list is a governance decision, not a technical one. It defines what the system can and cannot reference.
✓

Out-of-scope documents identified

Document categories that should not be indexed are named explicitly: billing records, HR files, personal communications, documents under active litigation hold, or sources outside the intended workflow scope.

Explicit exclusions prevent accidental ingestion of sensitive material into the retrieval corpus.
✓

Document format inventory

File types available for ingestion are identified: PDF, DOCX, CSV exports, XLSX, plain text. Format conversion requirements are flagged before installation.

Most practices have documents in 3–5 formats. Pre-conversion planning prevents delays during the indexing phase.
○

Document freshness review

Key documents (payer policies, protocols) are verified as current before indexing. Stale documents in the corpus create retrieval quality problems during the pilot.

Sentinel Node will surface document age warnings during retrieval — but catching stale docs before indexing is faster.
—

Document tagging strategy

Metadata tags for document type, source, version, and applicable workflow can be assigned during ingestion to improve retrieval precision. This can be configured during the pilot period.

Review Governance

✓

Named reviewer per workflow

Every workflow has an identified human reviewer: a specific staff member, RN, or licensed professional who is responsible for review and approval. "Someone will review it" is not acceptable.

The reviewer identity is recorded in the audit envelope. Unnamed review does not satisfy the governance requirement.
✓

Escalation path defined

When a staff reviewer encounters something outside their authority, there is a clear escalation target: supervising RN, attending physician, senior attorney, or compliance officer.

○

Review SLA agreed

Drafts that sit in the review queue for more than X hours are flagged or reassigned. This prevents workflow bottlenecks from appearing to be system performance issues.

Recommended: 4-hour SLA for time-sensitive workflows (prior auth), 24-hour for document review.
—

Reviewer training scheduled

30-minute walkthrough of the review interface, exception queue, citation badges, and audit export. Can occur during the pilot installation week.

IT Readiness

✓

Network placement decided

Where the appliance sits in the network: DMZ, dedicated VLAN, or isolated segment. Access controls to local document sources are confirmed.

The appliance needs read access to the corpus sources and write access to the audit log location. No internet access required for operation.
✓

Physical rack space confirmed

1U or 2U rack space is available in the server room or communications closet. Power and cooling requirements are within available capacity.

✓

IT contact named

A specific person at the practice or firm is the technical liaison for installation, network questions, and first-line support escalation.

○

Backup and retention policy reviewed

The appliance stores the local vector index and audit envelopes. Backup frequency, retention period, and storage location for audit records are decided before go-live.

—

Update window scheduled

A preferred maintenance window for software updates is identified. Updates are signed bundles delivered on customer-approved schedules — not automatic.

60-day proof-of-value structure.

The pilot has three phases with distinct objectives. Each phase ends with a checkpoint conversation before moving forward.

01
Days 1–14
Foundation: map, install, index, train
Workflow mapping session — confirm scope, task frequency, and success criteria with staff and leadership
Document corpus ingestion — index approved sources, verify retrieval quality against known queries
Physical appliance installation — network placement, access confirmation, audit log location
Reviewer training — 30-minute walkthrough of review interface, exception queue, citation system
Test query set — 10–20 real tasks run in supervised mode; retrieval quality assessed before production use
Checkpoint 1 — retrieval quality meets threshold? Reviewer comfortable? Any corpus gaps identified?
02
Days 15–45
Supervised production: real tasks, weekly cadence
Real workflow tasks processed through Sentinel Node — all drafts reviewed by named reviewer before use
Weekly check-in (30 min) — review exception log, corpus gaps, reviewer feedback, and time-tracking data
Exception queue review — which gaps appear repeatedly? Which documents need updating or adding?
Audit envelope sampling — spot-check that records are complete, retrievable, and attributable
Staff friction log — any workflow steps creating confusion or resistance are noted for resolution
Checkpoint 2 — is the retrieval quality stable? Are reviewers using it consistently? Any scope changes needed?
03
Days 46–60
Measurement and decision: evidence, outcomes, next step
Time-savings calculation — compare pre-pilot baseline to pilot-period actuals for the named workflow
Retrieval quality summary — exception rate, citation gap frequency, stale document flags, confidence distributions
Reviewer satisfaction assessment — structured questions to the review team about usability, trust, and efficiency
Audit completeness review — full 60-day audit trail sampled; any gaps documented
Corpus expansion readiness — which additional document sources would improve the next phase?
Stop/go decision meeting — leadership, IT, and clinical or legal reviewers present for the structured decision

Success criteria framework.

Agree on these criteria before the pilot begins. Measurement without a pre-agreed threshold is just observation — it does not produce a decision.

DimensionHow it is measuredSuggested threshold
Retrieval quality Reviewer accepts evidence set without significant manual correction on at least X% of tasks ≥ 80% acceptance rate
Time savings Average time per task compared to pre-pilot baseline for the same workflow ≥ 25% reduction
Exception rate Tasks routed to exception queue that require staff intervention before review ≤ 20% of tasks
Reviewer adoption Named reviewer uses system for ≥ X% of eligible tasks during the production phase ≥ 75% of eligible tasks
Audit completeness % of sessions with a complete, retrievable audit envelope 100% — no exceptions
Citation gap rate % of drafts with at least one blocked or flagged unsupported claim Tracked — no threshold (establishes corpus quality baseline)
Staff friction Reviewer-reported friction score (1–5) averaged over the production phase ≤ 2.5 average
Threshold note: the suggested thresholds above are starting points. Every practice and firm has different baseline conditions. Agreeing on thresholds before the pilot is more important than the specific numbers — the goal is a decision, not a scorecard.

The 60-day stop/go decision.

At the end of 60 days, three outcomes are realistic. Each has a clear operational interpretation and a defined next step.

✓ Expand

Retrieval quality, time savings, and reviewer adoption are at or above threshold. Audit record is complete. The pilot workflow is moved to steady-state operations. Additional workflows and/or additional document sources are scoped for the next phase.

  • Workflow moves to production governance
  • Corpus expansion plan drafted
  • Second workflow scoped
  • Annual support agreement discussed

⟲ Refine

Retrieval quality or reviewer adoption is below threshold but the operational concept is validated. Specific gaps are identified — typically corpus quality, document freshness, or reviewer training issues. A refinement period (30 additional days) with targeted fixes is justified.

  • Specific gaps documented
  • Corpus updates planned and executed
  • Reviewer retraining if indicated
  • 30-day extension with revised targets

✕ Stop

The workflow is not a fit for the current document environment, reviewer capacity, or operational structure. This is a legitimate outcome. A pilot that ends cleanly with documented reasons is more valuable than a deployment that continues without evidence. The audit record remains available to the practice.

  • Exit review documents specific failure modes
  • Corpus and audit records returned to practice
  • Recommendations for future readiness
  • No lock-in — hardware is returned

Workflow Experience Readiness

A successful pilot is not only a hardware installation. It is a staff-experience test. Before launch, Pocono AI and the pilot site identify which packet workflows will be tested.

Pilot workflow options:

  • Patient packet / pre-visit packet preparation
  • Prior authorization support packet
  • Denial response / appeal support packet
  • H&P / document chaos support summary
  • Referral and specialist-intake packet
  • Legal document workflow support (for law firm pilots)

Readiness checklist — workflow experience:

  • ✓ Named packet workflow selected
  • ✓ Staff role mapped to workflow
  • ✓ Reviewer role mapped
  • ✓ Approved source folders identified
  • ✓ Synthetic training case completed
  • ✓ Bad-output drill completed
  • ✓ Missing-evidence escalation route confirmed
  • ✓ Pilot metrics selected
  • ✓ Staff friction log owner named
