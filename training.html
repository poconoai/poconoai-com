Training Overview — Sentinel Node Pilot

Training is Part of the Safety Architecture

Sentinel Node is not simply installed. It is introduced through a controlled pilot-readiness process that trains users, defines safe-use boundaries, verifies local operation, and reinforces human review before trust is earned.

Sentinel Node produces decision-support drafts only. No output is final until reviewed, edited, approved, and owned by the responsible professional.

Section 1

Why Training Matters

AI in regulated environments is not plug-and-play. Training is not an optional add-on — it is part of the governance system.

Clinical and legal professionals carry personal liability for their decisions. An AI system that staff use without understanding what it is, what it is not, and what its outputs require of them creates risk — not relief. The goal of training is to prevent unsafe reliance, clarify professional responsibility, and teach every user how to treat Sentinel Node outputs as drafts that require human judgment before they are trusted or used.

Prevent unsafe reliance

Staff who do not understand what the system can and cannot do may over-trust outputs, skip citation checks, or treat a draft as a final decision. Training closes that gap.

Clarify professional responsibility

The physician, attorney, or responsible professional owns the output from the moment they approve it. Training ensures they understand that responsibility is not transferred to the AI system.

Reinforce human review as the default

Every user is trained on a single non-negotiable boundary: AI outputs are drafts. Review, edit, approve, and own them — or do not use them.

Training available during pilot deployment. Training is introduced during the pilot engagement, aligned with the specific workflow being piloted and the roles involved. It is not a one-size-fits-all module.
Section 2

Who Gets Trained

The pilot training program covers four role categories. These roles are intentionally simple for v1. Future versions may introduce more granular user roles, permissions, and workflow controls as Sentinel Node expands beyond the initial pilot environment.

Pilot Users

Staff who interact with Sentinel Node day-to-day: preparing queries, reviewing outputs, flagging issues, and following the safe-use workflow. May include medical assistants, paralegals, intake staff, prior-authorization staff, and office managers. Trained on what the system is, how to ask safe questions, how to check citations, and when to escalate.

Responsible Reviewer

The licensed professional who reviews, edits, approves, or rejects every AI-drafted output before it is used. The physician, attorney, or senior professional who owns the final output. Trained on output evaluation, citation verification, escalation, and the bad-output workflow.

Practice Administrator / Pilot Lead

The operational owner of the pilot. Responsible for monitoring the pilot, collecting observations, coordinating issue escalation, and completing the pilot reporting process. May sign the Training Acknowledgment Form on behalf of the pilot team for v1.

Installer / IT Support

The person or team responsible for physical placement, network configuration, and ongoing technical support of the Sentinel Node appliance. May be Pocono AI directly, the practice's own IT staff, an MSP partner, or a combination. Trained on installation assumptions, local operation verification, update procedures, and audit-log verification.

Note: v1 uses simple role categories. Future versions may introduce more granular user permissions, workflow controls, and audit boundaries as Sentinel Node scales beyond single-office pilots.

Customer Experience Training

What Staff Actually Do During the Pilot

Sentinel Node is not trained as a generic chatbot. It is trained as a governed workflow support system. During pilot onboarding, users learn what the system does on a normal workday: prepare cited packets, surface missing evidence, organize chaotic records, and route drafts to a responsible human reviewer.

The training goal is not to make staff “trust the AI.” The goal is to make staff trust the process: approved sources, bounded retrieval, visible citations, documented uncertainty, reviewer sign-off, and an audit trail that shows how the draft was produced.

Core process principle: Every packet produced by Sentinel Node is a draft. It is not a final clinical note, a submitted authorization, a signed appeal, or a complete medical record. A licensed professional reviews, edits, approves, and owns the final version before any professional use.

Module 1 — Patient Packet / Pre-Visit Packet

Current pain

Staff often search across EHR exports, scanned PDFs, labs, imaging, outside records, prior notes, medication history, and old messages. The information may exist — but it is scattered and hard to trust quickly.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user selects the approved patient record set and asks Sentinel Node to prepare a pre-visit packet. The system retrieves from local approved sources, drafts a structured packet, and cites the source material behind each important claim.

Packet may include

Reason for visit · Relevant diagnoses · Current medication context

Recent labs and imaging · Prior treatments · Specialist notes

Unresolved questions · Missing or stale documents · Source citations for review

Human review boundary. The packet is a draft. It is not a clinical note, order, diagnosis, or final medical judgment. The responsible clinician or trained reviewer verifies the sources, edits the packet, and owns the final version.

Training exercise

Give staff a synthetic patient chart. Ask them to produce a pre-visit packet, verify citations, identify missing records, and escalate at least one uncertain item before completing the drill.

Pilot measures

  • Time to packet
  • Missing-document flags
  • Reviewer corrections
  • Unsupported claims caught
  • Reviewer confidence
  • Staff friction log

Module 2 — Prior Authorization Support Packet

Current pain

Prior authorization work often fails because the supporting evidence is scattered. Staff must find diagnoses, dates, failed therapies, medication trials, conservative treatment history, imaging, labs, specialist notes, and payer-specific criteria — often under time pressure.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user asks Sentinel Node to prepare a prior authorization support packet. The system retrieves from local approved documents and drafts a structured packet for human review. The goal is first-submission completeness, not guaranteed approval.

Packet may include

Requested service, medication, imaging, or procedure · Diagnosis and relevant clinical history

Prior therapies tried and failed · Dates and source references · Supporting labs, imaging, or specialist notes

Medical-necessity draft language · Missing-evidence checklist · Reviewer sign-off area

Human review boundary. Sentinel Node does not submit the prior authorization, communicate with payers, make coverage arguments autonomously, or guarantee approval. It prepares draft support material from local evidence for staff and clinician review only.

Training exercise

Use a synthetic prior authorization scenario. Staff must assemble the packet, verify every citation, flag missing evidence, and route the draft to the responsible reviewer before completing the drill.

Pilot measures

  • Time per packet
  • First-submission completeness
  • Missing-evidence rate
  • Reviewer edits
  • Denial reason tracking
  • Staff friction log

Module 3 — Denial Response / Appeal Support Packet

Current pain

When a denial arrives, staff often restart the process manually. The denial letter, payer rationale, original submission, chart evidence, and next-step options may be disconnected. Assembling an appeal-ready packet from scratch under time pressure is where evidence gaps become critical.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user provides the denial reason and approved local source set. Sentinel Node prepares a denial response support packet with cited evidence and a missing-evidence checklist — an appeal-ready packet for the responsible professional to review and act on.

Packet may include

Denial reason summary · Payer’s stated basis · Evidence already submitted

Evidence missing from the first submission · Clinical timeline · Draft appeal support language

Supporting citations · Reviewer checklist · Human decision documented

Human review boundary. Sentinel Node does not decide whether to appeal, does not practice medicine or law, and does not provide insurance advocacy as an autonomous actor. It organizes evidence so the responsible professional can decide what to do.

Training exercise

Give staff a synthetic denial letter. Ask them to build a denial response packet, identify what was missing from the first submission, verify sources, and send the draft to the reviewer.

Pilot measures

  • Time to appeal-ready packet
  • Missing-document identification
  • Reviewer edits
  • Appeal readiness score
  • Final human decision documented

Module 4 — H&P / Document Chaos Support

Current pain

Clinical records are often long, duplicated, stale, fragmented, or copied forward. H&Ps, consult notes, hospital records, outside PDFs, medication lists, and test results can hide the facts that matter. Finding and trusting the current, relevant clinical facts takes significant time.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user asks Sentinel Node to prepare a cited documentation support summary from approved local records. The system retrieves relevant source material and identifies what appears current, stale, missing, or conflicting — grounded in local evidence, not AI memory.

Output may include

Key history elements · Relevant past medical history · Medication and allergy context

Prior procedures · Recent tests and results · Conflicting information flags

Missing information checklist · Suggested source list for reviewer verification · “Do not rely without review” warning

Human review boundary. The output is not an H&P by itself. It is not a signed note. It is a source-grounded draft support tool. The clinician owns the final documentation and must verify accuracy before any clinical or administrative use.

Training exercise

Use a synthetic chaotic chart with duplicate notes, stale medication history, and missing outside records. Staff and reviewer must identify what is reliable, what is stale, and what must be escalated before completing the drill.

Pilot measures

  • Time to usable summary
  • Unsupported claims caught
  • Stale records identified
  • Reviewer corrections
  • After-hours documentation friction
  • Staff confidence

Bad Output and Missing Evidence Drill

Every user must practice what happens when Sentinel Node produces an incomplete, uncertain, unsupported, stale, or wrong output. This drill is not optional.

Required workflow — no exceptions: Stop → Do not use → Identify the problem → Check the citation → Escalate to reviewer → Correct source set or prompt → Rerun if appropriate → Review again before use → Preserve in pilot friction log.
1

Stop

Do not use the output as final. Do not forward, file, or act on it.

2

Identify the problem

What is missing, stale, conflicting, unsupported, or uncertain? Be specific.

3

Check the cited source

Does the citation support the claim? If not, that is the specific finding to escalate.

4

Escalate to the responsible reviewer

Notify the Responsible Reviewer and Practice Administrator. They determine next steps.

5

Correct and rerun fresh

Correct the source set or prompt and rerun — never patch over a bad answer. Full review of the new output before any use.

6

Log the issue

Record in the pilot friction log. Do not suppress it. Documented pilot observations are how the system improves.

Training principle: Sentinel Node does not replace professional judgment. It makes evidence easier to find, package, verify, and audit. No user should quietly patch a bad output and treat it as validated.
Section 3

Installer Readiness

Version 1 assumes a single physical Sentinel Node appliance in one office. The installer may be Pocono AI directly, the practice's own IT person, an MSP partner, or a combination.

Installation training covers local placement, network assumptions, source ingestion, local operation verification, and the handoff checklist that must be completed before pilot use begins. The goal is to verify that the system is operating locally as designed before any professional output is generated.

No pilot use begins before a go/no-go readiness check. The system is not handed to end users until the installer has verified local operation, confirmed the audit log is functioning, and completed the pre-pilot handoff checklist.

High-Level Installer Readiness Checklist

  • Pre-install review: scope, approved sources, and network environment confirmed
  • Physical placement: appliance located in controlled access area
  • Local network assumptions: confirmed local-only operation before pilot
  • VLAN/firewall review: network segmentation appropriate for the deployment
  • Approved-source ingestion process: confirmed and tested with non-sensitive test documents
  • Local operation verification: confirmed no routine external data transfer
  • Update verification: system update path verified and understood
  • Audit-log verification: audit chain is functioning and entries are readable
  • Handoff checklist: completed and signed before pilot use begins
  • Go/no-go readiness check: final review before first end-user session

This is a public, buyer-facing summary. Detailed installation guidance is provided to qualified installers and MSP partners directly during the pilot engagement. Sensitive operational security details are not published publicly.

Section 4

End User Readiness

End user training is written for both non-technical staff and professional reviewers. It begins with what Sentinel Node is — and, critically, what it is not.

Non-technical staff

May include office managers, medical assistants, paralegals, intake staff, billing staff, and prior-authorization staff. These users prepare queries, work with approved source material, and flag outputs for professional review. They are trained to treat every output as a draft requiring a professional sign-off before use.

Professional reviewers

May include physicians, attorneys, practice owners, managing partners, and senior clinical or legal reviewers. These users hold the final professional responsibility for every output they approve. They are trained on citation verification, output evaluation, the escalation process, and the bad-output workflow.

End User Training Coverage

What Sentinel Node is

  • A local AI appliance that retrieves from approved documents and drafts decision-support materials
  • A system that requires a licensed professional to review and approve every output
  • A tool designed to support specific, bounded, pre-approved workflows
  • A system that maintains a reviewable audit log of every step

What Sentinel Node is not

  • A final decision-maker, clinical authority, or legal advisor
  • A substitute for physician judgment, attorney responsibility, or professional expertise
  • A system that can be trusted without checking citations and source references
  • A tool that produces outputs ready to send without professional review

How to ask safe questions

  • Use specific, scoped requests tied to a defined patient or matter
  • Reference only approved, in-scope source documents
  • Specify the output type expected (summary, draft letter, checklist, etc.)
  • Do not ask the system to make decisions — ask it to surface evidence

How to treat outputs

  • Treat every output as a draft requiring human review and professional approval
  • Check every citation against the actual source document before approval
  • Flag any unsupported claim, inconsistency, or missing information
  • Do not forward, send, or file an output without the responsible professional's approval
Section 5

Human Review Is Required

Sentinel Node does not make autonomous clinical, legal, financial, or operational decisions. Sentinel Node does not replace physician judgment, attorney judgment, or professional responsibility. No output should be sent to a patient, client, court, payer, opposing counsel, or external party unless reviewed and approved by the responsible professional.

Human review is not a formality. It is the system boundary. The professional who approves an output owns it — including responsibility for any action taken on the basis of that output.

Before approval

  • Read the full output, not just the summary
  • Check every cited source reference against the actual source document
  • Identify any claim that cannot be verified in the source material
  • Confirm the scope is correct and no out-of-scope records were referenced

After approval

  • The responsible professional has reviewed, edited as needed, and approved the output
  • The output may be used, filed, or transmitted in accordance with professional obligations
  • The audit record reflects the approved state and the reviewer's identity
  • The professional retains full accountability for the content

When to escalate without approval

  • The output contains a fact you cannot verify in the source records
  • The output is inconsistent with your professional knowledge of the case
  • The output is incomplete, uncertain, or does not address the question asked
  • Something about the output feels wrong — trust that judgment and escalate
Section 6

Bad or Uncertain Output Workflow

Core safety requirement: If an output is incomplete, uncertain, unsupported, inconsistent, or inappropriate, the user must document the issue, escalate to the responsible reviewer, and rerun only with corrected context or a fresh prompt. No output is final until reviewed, edited, approved, and owned by the responsible professional.

The required workflow is exactly: Document first → escalate to reviewer → rerun fresh.

1

Do not use the questionable output

Set the output aside. Do not forward, file, or act on it. Do not quietly correct a single wrong detail and treat the output as validated. A bad output requires a fresh process, not a patch.

2

Document the issue as a pilot observation

Record what happened: what was asked, what the output said, what was wrong or uncertain, and when it occurred. This is a pilot observation — it is valuable data that improves the system and demonstrates governance discipline. Do not suppress it.

3

Escalate to the responsible reviewer

Notify the designated Responsible Reviewer and Practice Administrator / Pilot Lead. They determine whether the issue warrants a workflow change, a source correction, a scope adjustment, or escalation to Pocono AI.

4

Rerun only with corrected context or a fresh prompt

If a rerun is appropriate, it begins with a fresh prompt, a clarified source set, or a corrected document context — not a patch on the original output. The new run goes through the full review process before any output is used.

Never quietly patch over a bad answer and treat it as validated. If the bad output workflow feels burdensome, that is the governance boundary working correctly. Regulated environments require documented evidence of how decisions were made and corrected, not just evidence of the final outcome.
Section 7

Medical and Legal Appendices

The universal training system is supported by two professional appendices. These are provided during the pilot engagement to the relevant professional team.

Medical Appendix

  • PHI awareness and HIPAA-aligned data handling during the pilot
  • Prior authorization and denial workflow support
  • Chart review support: what the system can and cannot surface
  • Care-team drafting boundaries and appropriate use cases
  • Patient communication: what requires direct physician authorship
  • No autonomous diagnosis, treatment, or prescribing decisions — ever

Legal Appendix

  • Attorney professional responsibility and AI-assisted work product
  • Privilege and confidentiality: what stays in the practice environment
  • Citation verification as a professional responsibility, not a system guarantee
  • Discovery support: output review before any production or use
  • Deposition preparation: what Sentinel Node can assist with and what it cannot
  • No unreviewed filings, legal submissions, or advice to clients without attorney sign-off

These appendices are provided as part of the pilot training materials, not published publicly in full. Contact Pocono AI to discuss specific appendix coverage for your practice type.

Section 8

Multi-Location Appendix

Version 1 starts with a single office and one physical appliance. Multi-location practices are not excluded — they require additional planning.

The multi-location appendix covers the additional governance requirements that arise when a practice has more than one physical location, multiple care teams, or distributed reviewer responsibilities. It is a planning document, not a deployment guide for v1.

Source custody

Which approved documents are accessible from which location. Cross-location data access must be explicitly scoped and governed, not assumed.

Access boundaries

Which users at which locations can access which patient or matter records. Role boundaries must be defined per location before multi-site deployment.

Network segmentation

Each location's Sentinel Node appliance operates on its own local network segment. Cross-location network assumptions require independent review.

Reviewer responsibility

Each location must have a designated Responsible Reviewer. Remote review across locations requires explicit workflow design and approval.

Support escalation

Each location's IT support path must be defined before deployment. Escalation routing differs by location type and IT support model.

v1 starting point

Every multi-location practice begins with a single-office pilot. Expansion to additional locations is planned after the first pilot demonstrates baseline readiness.

Section 9

Pilot Training Acknowledgment Form

Training documentation and sign-off may be used during pilot engagements. The Pilot Training Acknowledgment Form records the pilot team's awareness of the safe-use boundaries before or during deployment.

For v1, the practice owner, managing partner, or designated Practice Administrator / Pilot Lead may sign on behalf of the pilot team. The form is provided as part of the pilot engagement materials and is not required before a pilot conversation begins.

Acknowledgment Form Covers

  • The pilot team understands Sentinel Node outputs are decision-support drafts only
  • The pilot team understands outputs require human review before use
  • The pilot team understands citations and source material must be verified
  • The pilot team understands no output is final until approved by the responsible professional
  • The pilot team understands uncertain or bad outputs must be documented, escalated, and rerun fresh
  • The pilot team understands Sentinel Node is being evaluated in a pilot environment and is not a substitute for clinical, legal, or professional judgment
Important: The Training Acknowledgment Form is a governance and pilot-management tool. It does not constitute HIPAA compliance, SOC 2 readiness, clinical validation, legal approval, or regulatory clearance of any kind.
Section 10

Investor and Buyer Confidence

Regulated-industry AI adoption fails when systems are deployed without clear human boundaries, without documentation of what the AI is doing, and without training that makes safe use the default. Sentinel Node is designed to avoid all three failure modes.

We train before use.
We verify before trust.
We document before scale.

Sentinel Node is designed to make AI adoption slower where it must be slower, safer where it must be safer, and more useful only after the human review boundary is understood.

Train before use
Verify before trust
Document before scale

Training is not a barrier to adoption. It is how responsible AI adoption works. Every physician, attorney, staff member, and administrator who uses Sentinel Node enters the pilot understanding exactly what the system produces, what it requires of them, and what happens when something goes wrong.
