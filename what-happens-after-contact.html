Sentinel Node What Happens After You Reach Out

The engagement sequence, not a sales sequence.

When a practice or firm contacts Pocono AI, the first thing that happens is a working conversation about the specific workflow that brought you here. There is no SDR follow-up cadence. There is no automated nurture. There is no demo deck. There is a sequence of five working sessions, and at the end of them either you have a pilot plan you trust, or you don’t.

01 Week 0 60 minutes

Workflow assessment call

Marc and one technical lead, on a call with you and whoever from your team owns the workflow you want to fix. We talk through the specific pain point that brought you here — a prior auth backlog, a documentation burden, a discovery review bottleneck, a policy retrieval problem.

  • Identify the one workflow you want a Sentinel Node to handle first.
  • Map the inputs that feed it — documents, policies, EHR exports, case files.
  • Surface the governance and audit requirements your domain imposes.
  • Determine whether the workflow is a fit for the architecture — if not, we say so.

Outcome: A written one-page summary of the workflow, its inputs, its current cost, and whether it’s a candidate for a pilot.

02 Week 1 ~2 weeks

Infrastructure review

Your IT lead (or whoever runs your network) and our technical lead walk through the appliance’s deployment requirements against your environment. This is engineering work, not procurement theatre.

  • Network: where the Sentinel Node lives, what it talks to, what it doesn’t.
  • Storage: throughput, retention policy for audit and case data.
  • Identity: how clinicians or attorneys authenticate against the node.
  • Security & governance alignment: HIPAA posture, BAA, audit access, breach response.
  • Constraints on updates, model bundles, and signed-release acceptance.

Outcome: A deployment posture document specific to your environment, with a list of anything that needs adjustment before installation.

03 Week 2–3 ~1 week

Workflow refinement & gate planning

We sit down with the people who actually do the work and refine the workflow against the gates the system imposes. Where does the citation gate fit? Where does human review escalate? Who can sign and who can’t? What constitutes “done”?

  • Map each step of the workflow to a Sentinel Node state — retrieval, drafting, gate, review, sign-off.
  • Define escalation rules: when must the system refuse to act and hand to a human?
  • Define the source library: what gets ingested, what gets versioned, what gets reviewed.
  • Identify the reviewers, their credentials, their roles in the audit record.

Outcome: A workflow specification that names the gates, the reviewers, the sign-off authority, and the audit fields the pilot will capture.

04 Week 3–5 ~2 weeks

Pilot deployment preparation

The appliance is provisioned. Your initial document corpus is ingested. Reviewers are onboarded. The workflow specification from stage 03 is implemented end to end on the node.

  • Sentinel Node provisioned and installed on your network.
  • Initial corpus ingested — policies, case files, protocols, whatever the workflow needs.
  • Reviewer accounts created, credentials bound to the audit record.
  • Pilot kickoff brief: who’s using it, what they’re measuring, what success looks like.
  • Support expectations set: response times, escalation path, who picks up the phone.

Outcome: A working appliance loaded with your real material, ready for the first reviewers to use on real cases.

05 Week 5–12 ongoing

Collaborative operational refinement

The reviewers run real cases on the node. Marc and the technical lead sit close to the practice during the pilot — not as vendors waiting for tickets, but as people refining the workflow alongside the clinicians and attorneys using it.

  • Weekly working sessions with the reviewers using the node.
  • Continuous refinement: retrieval tuning, gate sensitivity, escalation thresholds.
  • Governance tuning: what is logged, what is exported, what is reviewed by compliance.
  • Adverse-event review: any case where the system did the wrong thing is dissected together.
  • A weekly pilot report summarising what went through the node, what got escalated, what got signed.

Outcome: A pilot record — cases handled, gates triggered, escalations resolved, sign-offs completed — that the practice or firm owns and can show to a board, an insurer, or a regulator.

You can stop at any stage

If after Stage 01 we don’t think it’s a fit, we say so. If after Stage 02 you decide the deployment posture doesn’t work for you, you walk away with the posture document and no obligation.

You talk to the same people throughout

There is no account team handoff. Marc and the technical lead who do Stage 01 are the same people who do Stage 05. The phone number is the same. The email is the same.

Nothing is committed until Stage 04

The first three stages are working sessions. No appliance ships, no money changes hands, no production data is touched, until you have a signed workflow specification you actually trust.

What this engagement is not

  • A demo loop. We don’t replay the same canned scenarios across calls.
  • A sales sequence with stages mapped to a CRM. There is no CRM.
  • A pitch deck. The deliverables above are working documents, not slides.
  • An RFP exercise. If you need one, we can support it — but it isn’t how we lead.
  • A vendor evaluation theatre. We don’t do bake-offs against tools that aren’t architecturally comparable.

Ready for the first working conversation?

The fastest path is the pilot brief — a short framework questionnaire that lets us turn the first call into a working session instead of an introductory one. If you’d rather start with a direct email, that’s also fine.
