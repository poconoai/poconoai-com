The Governance Receipt

A Chain-of-Custody Framework for Accountable AI in Healthcare and Law

AI governance is moving from policy to proof. In healthcare and law, a fluent answer is not enough. Every consequential AI-assisted output needs a chain of custody: what was asked, what evidence was retrieved, what the system generated, who reviewed it, what changed, and whether sensitive data stayed local.

The Governance Receipt — Eight Layers

IdentityWho initiated and authenticated the event
TaskPrompt, workflow, patient or matter context
EvidenceRetrieved records, passages, policy versions
GenerationModel, parameters, draft output produced
ReviewNamed reviewer, decisions, edits, timestamp
OutputFinal message, letter, or export record
Audit TrailTamper-evident event log for reconstruction
Data BoundaryWhere data stayed; third-party transmission status

No governance receipt, no accountable AI.

Black box vs. governance receipt

The governance difference is easiest to see as a contrast between a black-box workflow and a receipt-producing workflow.

Black-Box AI

  • Prompt → answer
  • No stable record of evidence used
  • Human review may be informal
  • Data path may be hard to prove
  • Useful, but difficult to defend

Governance-Receipt AI

  • Identity → task → evidence → draft → review → output → audit trail
  • Retrieved documents and citations preserved
  • Reviewer decision is captured
  • Data boundary is recorded
  • Useful and reconstructable

A receipt does not guarantee perfection. It prevents blindness. When something matters, the organization can reconstruct the event instead of relying on memory, screenshots, or assumptions.

Grounded in established standards

The Governance Receipt framework aligns with published guidance from authoritative sources:

  • NIST AI RMF 1.0 and AI 600-1 — trustworthy AI requires accountability, transparency, privacy, security, and explainability.
  • HIPAA Security Rule, 45 C.F.R. § 164.312(b) — systems using electronic protected health information require audit controls.
  • HHS OCR guidance on third-party tracking and disclosure risks for regulated entities.
  • ABA Formal Opinion 512 — lawyers using generative AI must consider competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, and reasonable fees.

Policies say what people are supposed to do. A governance receipt shows what actually happened.
