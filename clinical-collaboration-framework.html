Sentinel Node Clinical Collaboration Framework

Physician-guided workflows without overstated clinical claims.

Sentinel Node is designed to be shaped with clinicians, not imposed on them. This page explains how physicians, nurses, practice managers, and compliance reviewers participate during pilots — and what Pocono AI does not claim.

Why this matters

Clinical AI fails when the workflow is designed away from the people who carry the liability.

The collaboration model is intentionally practical. Pocono AI is not asking clinicians to endorse broad AI claims. We ask them to help define safe use boundaries, reviewer roles, evidence sources, escalation points, and the conditions under which an output should be blocked rather than polished.

The clinical question

Where can a local evidence-retrieval appliance reduce administrative burden without crossing into autonomous clinical decision-making?

The governance answer

Use Sentinel Node for bounded retrieval, draft preparation, policy comparison, and audit traceability — with licensed human review as the required final gate.

Buyer translation: clinical collaboration is not a marketing advisory board. It is a pilot operating discipline: define the workflow, test the friction, tighten the gate, document the exceptions, and only then expand the use case.

Five collaboration lanes during a pilot.

Each lane creates a concrete artifact the customer can review. The goal is not “AI adoption.” The goal is operational fit under professional supervision.

01 · Workflow advisory

Clinicians and operators identify the first high-friction workflow. Examples include prior authorization appeals, denial packet preparation, policy retrieval, chart summarization for administrative use, and internal protocol lookup.

02 · Source boundary

The customer decides what the system is allowed to know. Approved source folders, policies, forms, letters, SOPs, and exports are defined before indexing. Ambiguous or excluded sources stay out.

03 · Review gate design

Reviewer roles are mapped before the first live workflow. Who may approve? Who may reject? When is escalation required? What must be cited before a draft can move forward?

04 · Pilot feedback loop

Users report misses, confusing retrieval, unsafe phrasing, workflow friction, and false confidence. Those signals are used to tighten prompts, retrieval scope, training language, and governance policy.

05 · Expansion decision

Only after evidence from the first workflow is reviewed do we discuss expansion. The next workflow should be earned by operational clarity, not assumed because a demo looked good.

What collaborators review.

The best clinical feedback is specific. The pilot should produce reviewable objects rather than vague impressions.

1

Workflow map

Current-state steps, handoffs, bottlenecks, documents, and decision points.

2

Evidence set

The approved documents and source classes Sentinel Node may retrieve from.

3

Draft behavior

What the system may summarize, draft, compare, or route — and what it may not say.

4

Block conditions

Missing citation, conflicting evidence, high-risk wording, wrong document class, or reviewer unavailable.

5

Audit artifact

Reviewer action, source references, final status, and traceable record for the pilot workflow.

Explicit anti-claims.

This page is deliberately restrained because regulated buyers need clear boundaries. Pocono AI should not sound like it is selling autonomous medicine.

What this is not

  • Not autonomous diagnosis.
  • Not a substitute for physician judgment.
  • Not an FDA-cleared medical device claim.
  • Not a promise of clinical outcome improvement.
  • Not automatic patient communication without review.

What this is

  • A local governed retrieval and drafting appliance.
  • A way to reduce administrative search and assembly burden.
  • A human-in-the-loop workflow system.
  • A pilot framework for measuring fit before expansion.
  • An auditable operating path for regulated teams.
Founder discipline: the stronger the boundaries, the more credible the product becomes. Sentinel Node should win because it respects professional judgment, not because it pretends to replace it.

The pilot question.

A clinical collaboration pilot should end with a clear operational answer.

Did it reduce search and assembly burden?Measured through time-to-draft, staff touchpoints, and rework reduction.
Did reviewers trust the citations?Measured through citation usefulness, missing-source rates, and blocked-output events.
Did governance hold under pressure?Measured through review compliance, audit completeness, and escalation behavior.
