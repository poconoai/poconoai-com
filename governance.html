Sentinel Node AI Governance

AI Verification & Governance
for Regulated Workflows

Why human review, provenance, traceability, and local control are not optional features for medical practices, law firms, and compliance-sensitive organizations — and how the Sentinel Node™ operationalizes each of them.

The Problem

Most AI Tools Aren't Designed for Regulated Environments

General-purpose AI tools — the kind pitched to every industry — are optimized for speed and ease of use. They were not designed with the accountability requirements of a medical practice or a law office in mind.

In regulated environments, the question is not just does the AI give a good answer — it's:

  • Where did that answer come from? Which documents, which sources, which version?
  • Who reviewed it before it was acted on? Was a qualified professional in the loop?
  • Is there a record of the interaction? Can it be reconstructed later if challenged?
  • Where did my patient or client data go? Which vendor's servers? Which country?
  • Can the vendor see my data? Is it used for training? Shared with third parties?

Cloud-based AI tools typically cannot answer these questions satisfactorily. The Sentinel Node™ is built specifically to answer them — by design, not by add-on.

The governance gap in AI adoption: Most AI tools that reach healthcare and legal workflows were not built for those workflows. The risks they introduce — provenance gaps, unreviewed outputs, data exfiltration, audit holes — are not theoretical. They are the natural result of applying general-purpose tools to regulated contexts.

The Four Pillars

What Responsible AI Governance Requires

Across healthcare, law, and other regulated industries, four properties define whether an AI system can be responsibly deployed:

1. Human Review

No AI output should enter a clinical record, legal document, or operational workflow without review by a qualified professional. The AI is a draft tool. The human is the decision-maker.

2. Provenance

Every AI-assisted output must be traceable to its source documents. Which file? Which page? Which section? Without provenance, an AI answer cannot be verified or challenged.

3. Traceability

There must be a durable record of every AI interaction: who asked, what was retrieved, what the model produced, and who reviewed it. This record must be reviewable and retained.

4. Local Control

Sensitive data — patient records, case files, privileged communications — must not leave your facility. The organization using the tool must control the data, not the vendor. In a regionally governed deployment, local control is also jurisdictional control: the node operates under the compliance framework of the region where it is installed, not the region where the vendor is headquartered.

The Sentinel Node™ operationalizes all four. Each is a structural property of the system, not a configuration option or an add-on module.

Human Review

Why AI Must Never Act Without a Human in the Loop

The most consequential decision in any AI governance framework is this one: does the AI output go directly into a workflow, or does a qualified human review it first?

In medicine, an unreviewed AI output that enters a clinical note or influences a prescribing decision can harm a patient. In law, an unreviewed AI-generated research summary that shapes legal strategy can harm a client. These are not edge cases. They are the expected failure mode of systems that remove human judgment from the loop.

The Sentinel Node™ enforces human-in-the-loop review structurally. The user interface presents every AI response as a draft. The audit log records the review step. Staff cannot route an AI output into a workflow without it passing through a human decision point.

This is not a restriction on usefulness. It is a recognition of where AI is and is not reliable today — and a commitment to protecting the people your organization serves.

Provenance

Every Answer Has a Source — and You Can See It

Provenance is the ability to trace an AI answer back to the exact documents, pages, and passages that informed it. Most AI systems do not provide this. They return an answer — but not a citation trail.

In a regulated environment, this is not good enough. If a physician asks an AI about a drug interaction and the AI returns an answer, the physician needs to know: is this from my current formulary? My protocol manual? A document that was current last year but has since been updated?

The Sentinel Node™ uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture powered by Qdrant. When a query is processed, the system retrieves the most relevant document chunks from your local knowledge base and logs the source of every chunk: document ID, filename, page number, and section heading. The retrieved chunks become part of the prompt context — and part of the permanent audit record.

The result: every answer is grounded in identifiable source documents that were in your system at a specific point in time. This is traceable. This is auditable. This is provenance.

Why this matters for attorneys: Work product privilege and professional responsibility rules require attorneys to exercise independent judgment. AI-assisted research that cannot be traced to verifiable sources creates a professional responsibility risk that provenance-based systems directly mitigate.

Traceability

A Durable, Reviewable Record of Every AI Interaction

Traceability means that months or years after an AI-assisted interaction, you can reconstruct exactly what happened: what was asked, what was retrieved, what the model produced, and who reviewed it.

This is what regulators, malpractice investigators, and bar disciplinary boards may eventually ask for. "We use AI" is not a sufficient answer. "Here is the complete, reviewable log of every AI interaction, with source provenance and human review records" is.

The Sentinel Node™ audit log provides this. Today, in the pilot, every event is written to a local SQLite database as an append-only, reviewable record — nothing is modified or deleted in place during the retention window. The next audit-layer release (Stage 2) adds SHA-256 hash chaining and signed records so that altering a past entry breaks the chain and is detectable on verification. The log is retained for a minimum of 90 days, with configurable extension for organizational compliance requirements.

For investors and diligence reviewers, this also matters: an organization that can demonstrate structured AI governance — with audit trails — is meaningfully differentiated from one that cannot.

Local Control

Your Data Doesn't Leave. Not Even the Audit Log.

Cloud-based AI tools create a structural problem for regulated organizations: the vendor has access to your data. Sometimes contractually constrained, sometimes not. Sometimes used for model training. Sometimes shared with sub-processors in ways that a BAA or DPA does not fully address.

The Sentinel Node™ eliminates this problem at the architectural level. The inference model, the vector database, the retrieval pipeline, and the audit log all run on hardware inside your facility. There is no cloud hop. No vendor server. No third-party sub-processor receiving your queries or your patient data.

The audit log itself — the record of every AI interaction — is also local. You own it. You control access to it. If you are asked to produce it for a regulatory audit or a legal proceeding, you produce it directly, without coordinating with a vendor.

Air-gapped operation: The Sentinel Node™ is designed to operate without an internet connection once deployed. For organizations in high-sensitivity environments — correctional health, substance use treatment, psychiatric care, privilege-sensitive legal matters — this matters.

Practical Scenarios

What Governance Looks Like in Practice

Medical Practice

Prior Authorization Documentation

A nurse practitioner uses the Sentinel Node™ to draft prior authorization language for a specialty medication. The system retrieves relevant passages from the practice's clinical protocol manual and the current payer criteria document. The retrieved chunks and the generated draft are logged in the audit record. The supervising physician reviews the draft, confirms clinical accuracy against the cited sources, and approves it for submission. The entire interaction — query, sources, draft, reviewer, timestamp — is preserved in the reviewable log.

Law Firm

Case Research Under Privilege

An associate uses the Sentinel Node™ to research relevant case law from the firm's internal matter files. The system retrieves relevant case summaries and deposition excerpts from the local Qdrant database. The associate reviews the AI-synthesized summary, verifies the cited source documents, and confirms the analysis before integrating it into a brief. No query or document content ever left the firm's network. The full retrieval and review chain is logged locally.

Compliance & Investor Diligence

Demonstrating AI Governance to a Reviewer

A healthcare organization using the Sentinel Node™ is asked by a compliance officer or external auditor to demonstrate how AI is governed in their clinical workflows. They produce the local audit log — a complete, reviewable audit record of every AI interaction over the review period — along with documentation of the human review policy. The log shows source provenance for every retrieved document, every prompt, every response, and every reviewer. This is governance that can be demonstrated, not just asserted.

Runtime Governance · Sentinel Node v1.46

Governance Is Expressed as Runtime Gates

Governance is not a PDF afterthought. In the Sentinel Node, governance is expressed as runtime gates: OCR confidence, evidence admissibility, scope filtering, draft quality review, professional approval, export blocking, signed update custody, and local audit envelopes. An internal release-candidate build sequence turned these gates from policy language into observable controls. Each gate produces an auditable signal a reviewer can inspect. This is a pilot-candidate scaffold, not a production certification — professional review remains mandatory.

Evidence Train

Citation IDs, source hashes, and page or chunk metadata travel with every drafted claim, so a reviewer can trace each statement back to an approved source. Source data must still be selected correctly.

Audit Envelope

Login, ingestion, OCR gate, retrieval, generation, approval, export, and signed-update events are recorded with actor and scope. Document text, prompts, drafts, and tokens are excluded from audit metadata.

Named Reviewer

A draft-only workflow requires a named professional approval before any regulated output moves forward. The export gate blocks unapproved artifacts. No autopilot mode exists.

Draft-Quality Monitoring

Unsupported-claim warnings flag draft statements that lack admissible evidence. A reviewer interprets the warnings; the monitor does not prove correctness and does not replace professional judgment.

Drift Watch & Model Refresh

Drift Watch surfaces unsupported-claim rate, OCR rejection rate, image-fallback rate, zero-evidence task rate, and average retrieval score, with model-refresh readiness thresholds. These indicate risk and trend; they do not prove correctness.

Golden-Case Regression

Synthetic golden cases verify expected sources, blocked wrong-scope retrieval, rejected bad OCR, and flagged unsupported claims, with local run records. Golden cases are regression checks, not certification.

Local Metrics Dashboard

A local-only dashboard aggregates posture signals for a pilot lead, MSP, or compliance reviewer. It is not telemetry by default, does not upload metrics, and contains no PHI, privileged content, prompts, drafts, or identifiers.

Compliance Evidence Binder

An internal readiness and evidence map links each control to the questions reviewers ask, with status and limitations. It is not a HIPAA, legal, NIST, ISO, or production-deployment certification.

The OCR confidence gate defaults to 95%. Low-confidence material is refused or routed to image fallback or manual review rather than fabricated as text. Cloud AI is off unless explicitly approved by the customer; the default posture is local-only.

Honest Scope

What the Sentinel Node™ Governance Layer Does Not Do

We want to be specific about what this system provides and what falls outside its scope.

  • It does not certify HIPAA compliance for your organization. HIPAA compliance is an organizational program, not a product feature. The Sentinel Node™ supports HIPAA-aligned architecture for the AI interaction layer. Full compliance requires administrative safeguards, physical security, workforce training, and legal review by qualified counsel.
  • It does not substitute for professional judgment. The system is designed to support licensed professionals — not to replace them. Clinical and legal outputs require expert review before use.
  • It does not guarantee correctness of AI outputs. Large language models can produce plausible but incorrect responses. Human review is required precisely because AI is not infallible.
  • It does not provide legal advice. This page and the Pocono AI platform are not sources of legal or regulatory guidance. Consult qualified legal counsel for your organization's specific compliance obligations.
