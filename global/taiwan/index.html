The care coordination challenge in Taiwan’s super-aged society

Taiwan is projected to become a super-aged society, with more than 20% of the population over 65. Chronic disease, multi-morbidity, and long-term care needs are growing faster than clinical and caregiver capacity. The patient story is fragmented across hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, community care, and family caregivers — and no single actor can see the whole picture.

Where the patient story is lost

  • Post-discharge information does not reliably reach the community clinic or home care provider
  • Medication lists from multiple prescribers are not consistently reconciled
  • Family caregivers carry clinical information in memory rather than in structured, shareable records
  • Follow-up visit preparation requires manually reconstructing a patient’s recent hospital, clinic, pharmacy, and community care history
  • Long-term care documentation and handoff packets are labour-intensive to assemble

Where clinician time is lost

  • Preparing post-discharge follow-up visit summaries from hospital discharge notes, medication lists, and prior chronic-care records
  • Reconciling multiple medication sources before a chronic-care visit
  • Coordinating between primary care, specialists, long-term care, and community services
  • Explaining clinical history to family caregivers in a form they can use
  • Assembling care transition packets that support continuity rather than just handoff

Why imported black-box AI is not the answer

PDPA and data sovereignty

Taiwan’s Personal Data Protection Act governs sensitive health information. Routine transfer of patient records to offshore AI inference services requires careful legal analysis under Taiwanese law.

TFDA and SaMD framework

AI software that informs clinical decisions may engage the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration’s AI/ML medical device software framework. Human professional review must remain central.

Cultural and linguistic context

Taiwan’s clinical documentation, family communication, and long-term care coordination operate in Traditional Chinese. AI systems trained primarily on English-language data carry embedded assumptions that do not transfer.

NHI and My Health Bank integration

Taiwan’s National Health Insurance system and My Health Bank provide rich data infrastructure. AI that works alongside these systems, rather than replacing them or creating parallel data streams, is more appropriate.

Family caregiver reality

Caregiving in Taiwan often involves family members coordinating care across formal and informal settings. AI systems must support, not bypass, the caregiver relationship and the clinical oversight behind it.

Small clinic and community care economics

Much of Taiwan’s chronic and long-term care is delivered through small clinics, community health centres, and home care services. Enterprise-scale AI economics do not fit this reality.

First pilot concept: post-discharge and chronic-care coordination packets

The Sentinel Node appliance sits inside the clinic or community care setting, retrieves only from approved records in scope, drafts coordination packets from cited evidence, and requires the physician to review and approve before any output is used or shared.

1. Define scope

One clinic or long-term care setting. One coordination workflow (e.g. post-discharge follow-up visit preparation). Approved records in scope. Defined reviewer. Clear output type.

2. Local ingestion

Approved records from hospital discharge, prior clinic visits, pharmacy records, and community care notes enter the local system with full audit of what was received and indexed.

3. Evidence retrieval

For each follow-up visit preparation, the system retrieves: discharge summary, medication list, prior chronic-care records, specialist letters, and community care notes.

4. Local LLM drafting

The local model drafts a structured post-discharge coordination packet or chronic-care visit summary — from retrieved evidence only, with no external data and no cloud inference.

5. Citation verification

The draft is checked against source records. Missing medication reconciliation facts, absent discharge instructions, or unsupported care recommendations are flagged before physician review.

6. Physician review and approval

The physician reviews, edits, approves, or rejects. No packet is used, shared, or filed until a licensed clinician authorises it. Every decision is logged in the reviewable audit chain.

Governance and regulatory alignment

Relevant Taiwan frameworks

  • Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) — sensitive health data handling
  • TFDA AI/ML SaMD guidance — AI software in clinical decision contexts
  • MOHW Long-Term Care 3.0 — policy direction for long-term care coordination
  • NHI / My Health Bank / MediCloud — national health data infrastructure context
  • MOHW and NHIA — national health insurance and care coordination standards

Pocono AI architecture alignment

  • No routine offshore data transfer
  • Physician review required before any clinical use or sharing
  • Reviewable audit log of every retrieval and decision (tamper-evident: Stage 2)
  • Citation verification before human review
  • Missing facts flagged, not invented
  • Local data custody within the clinic or care setting

Taiwan legal, PDPA, and clinical regulatory review is required before any deployment. Local qualified counsel, clinical leadership, and MOHW/TFDA-awareness review must be engaged.

What a pilot would measure

Time saved per follow-up visit

Time spent preparing post-discharge or chronic-care visits before and after deployment.

Post-discharge packet completeness

Whether the coordination packet contains all required elements for safe continuity of care.

Medication reconciliation clarity

How completely and accurately medications from multiple sources are represented.

Missing-information events

How often preparation identifies a missing record, result, or clinical fact from the care history.

Caregiver understanding

Whether family caregivers receiving the coordination summary can understand and act on it.

Follow-up completion

Whether patients complete recommended follow-up steps identified in the coordination packet.

Unsupported claim rate

How often the draft includes claims not grounded in retrieved source records.

Audit trail completeness

Whether every step from request to approved output can be reconstructed from the audit log.
