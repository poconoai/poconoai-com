Veteran Node

Consent-first coordination infrastructure for the veteran ecosystem

The veteran ecosystem is full of people and organizations trying to help — VSOs, nonprofits, employers, churches, housing partners, funders, and more. But the ecosystem is fragmented. Handoffs break. Referrals go stale. Opportunities are missed. Veterans are often left carrying the whole map alone. Veteran Node helps organize the situation, surface next steps, and coordinate trusted handoffs — with consent, transparency, and human review at every step.

Why this matters

A fragmented ecosystem leaving too much on the veteran.

Transition from service is complex. So is navigating VA processes, disability claims, employment, housing, education benefits, community support, and the financial and health decisions that come with all of it. The organizations that want to help — VSOs, nonprofits, churches, employers, training programs, local businesses, and community groups — often don't know each other and can't coordinate.

Referrals happen on paper or over the phone, then disappear. Deadlines get missed because no one had a clear picture of what was due. Volunteers don't know where they're needed. Veterans who deserve introductions never get them because the right person didn't know to ask.

Veteran Node's job is not to solve this with automation. Its job is to help the humans in the ecosystem — veterans, advocates, VSO staff, coaches, and partners — see the situation clearly, identify next steps, and make introductions they've agreed to make, with full transparency about why.

What it does

Eight capabilities. One working slice. All governed.

Release Z2.01 ships the first working ecosystem-intelligence layer. Every capability below is live in the current build, using synthetic demo data. All recommendations are drafts. All introductions require consent. Human review is required on every Action Card.

🎯
Action Cards

One clear next step at a time

The system reviews available information — notes, open needs, funding deadlines, stale referrals — and surfaces one proposed next step. Every card shows why it matters, how urgent it is, how confident the system is, and exactly which records it's based on. Every card is a draft. Every card asks for human review before anything happens.

📋
Governance Receipts

Transparency in every match and decision

Every Action Card comes with a receipt: what was matched and why, what boundaries apply, whether consent is required and whether it's been confirmed, and whether human review is still pending. Nothing is hidden behind the recommendation. What you see is what the system did.

🤝
Trusted Handoffs

Eight-state auditable introduction workflow

When an Action Card involves introducing a veteran to an organization, it becomes a Trusted Handoff — an auditable workflow with eight states: draft → consent needed → ready for review → introduced → in progress → follow-up needed → complete / closed. An introduction cannot happen without confirmed consent. This is enforced in code, not just policy.

🗺️
Veteran Situation Picture

Goals, needs, and next actions in one view

A single view covering goals, open needs, upcoming deadlines, trusted contacts, active handoffs, and gaps in the profile — plus an Opportunity Radar surfacing relevant funding, employment and training opportunities, local organizations, trusted businesses, and accessibility programs.

📡
Opportunity Radar

Ranked next-best-action feed

Part of the Situation Picture: a ranked feed of Action Cards, each with its Governance Receipt one click away. The radar covers funding opportunities, employment and training paths, local organizations, trusted businesses, and accessibility programs — all flagged as "worth human review," never as determined or approved.

🏢
Organization Ecosystem View

The same intelligence from the other side

VSOs, nonprofits, employers, and partners see open needs by category, stuck and consent-blocked handoffs, suggested partnerships between organizations that don't yet know each other, volunteer shortages, and synthetic outcome metrics. Individual veteran detail is hidden by default everywhere in this view — shown only where consent has been explicitly confirmed.

Fully fictional, clearly marked, testable end-to-end

The current build includes a complete synthetic ecosystem: Jordan Hale's demo profile plus two additional veteran personas, six organizations (VSO, transition-support nonprofit, employment partner, housing partner, a church volunteer group, a veteran-business network), four trusted businesses, four funding opportunities, four volunteer needs, and six referral handoffs in different states. All fictional. All clearly marked. No real veteran data.

Next Best Actions

Deterministic, explainable, conservative by design

Matching is rule-based — region, category, keyword, and eligibility-tag overlap — deliberately deterministic and explainable rather than a semantic or LLM match. Thin or oddly-phrased data produces a low-confidence card, not a confident one. The system says "worth human review," never "determined" or "endorsed."

Consent-first by design

Five principles that are not policy — they are enforcement.

"Consent-first" is easy to write on a website. These are the specific, technical, verifiable ways Veteran Node implements it.

  • 1

    Every recommendation is a draft. No Action Card, match, or referral takes effect on its own. The system surfaces options; a human decides what happens next.

  • 2

    Every Action Card has a Governance Receipt. The receipt shows exactly what the system matched, why, what boundaries apply, whether consent has been confirmed, and whether human review is pending. Nothing is hidden.

  • 3

    Every introduction requires human review. A Trusted Handoff cannot advance from "ready for review" to "introduced" without a human making that call. The workflow enforces it.

  • 4

    An introduction cannot happen without confirmed consent. This is enforced in code. The system will reject the attempt to introduce without consent being recorded. It is not a guideline — it is a gate.

  • 5

    Organization views hide individual veteran detail by default. In the Organization Ecosystem View, a veteran's personal details are not shown unless consent has been explicitly confirmed for that specific relationship. Organizations see patterns and needs — not exposed profiles.

Explicit boundaries

What Veteran Node is not.

The framing of this product invites the question directly. Here are the boundaries, stated plainly and without hedging.

✕

Not surveillance. Veteran Node does not monitor, track, or observe veterans without their knowledge or involvement.

✕

Not background checking. It does not access, query, or report on criminal, financial, employment, or government records.

✕

Not covert collection. It reasons over local and imported data only. No scraping, no covert aggregation, no third-party data brokers.

✕

Not a VA benefits determination. Veteran Node has no affiliation with the Department of Veterans Affairs and makes no determination about benefit eligibility, claims status, or entitlement.

✕

Not legal, medical, or financial advice. Nothing produced by Veteran Node constitutes legal counsel, medical guidance, financial planning, or professional advice of any kind.

✕

Not a crisis or emergency response system. If you or someone you know is in crisis, please contact the Veterans Crisis Line: call or text 988, then press 1.

✕

Not a live web crawler. It does not browse the internet, pull live data feeds, or query external services autonomously.

✕

Not an autonomous agent. It never contacts a veteran, organization, employer, or anyone else on its own. Every recommendation is a draft. Every introduction requires a human and confirmed consent.

✕

Not an endorsement engine. Organization and business matches are surfaced as "worth human review" — never as guaranteed, approved, recommended, or endorsed partners.

Current release — Z2.01

What exists now. Stated plainly.

Pocono AI publishes what it has and what it doesn't claim. Here is the honest state of Veteran Node as of release Z2.01.

What is working now (Z2.01)

  • Action Cards, Governance Receipts, and Trusted Handoffs are built and running end-to-end.
  • Veteran Situation Picture, Opportunity Radar, and Next Best Actions feed are functional.
  • Organization Ecosystem View is active with default-hidden veteran detail and consent gating.
  • Synthetic demo ecosystem: Jordan Hale + 2 veteran personas, 6 organizations, 4 trusted businesses, 4 funding opportunities, 4 volunteer needs, 6 referral handoffs in various states.
  • All data in the current build is synthetic and fictional. No real veteran data is present.
  • 1,844 regression tests. Zero new failures against this release.
  • Compatibility confirmed: Medical, Law, Engineering, Home, Living Echo, and Enterprise Continuity modes are unaffected.

Known limitations (honest disclosures)

  • Trusted Handoffs currently live in memory for the running session. A restart resets any handoff created during the session back to its synthetic starting state. Persistent storage is the recommended next step.
  • Matching is rule-based (region, category, keyword, eligibility-tag overlap) — deliberately deterministic and explainable. Thin or ambiguous data produces a low-confidence card, not a confident one. No LLM-based semantic matching yet.
  • Consent is currently a single flag plus a handoff's state history — not yet a dedicated, append-only consent ledger. A formal ledger is the recommended next step.

Recommended next steps (engineering roadmap)

  • Persistent handoff and receipt storage (database-backed, not session memory).
  • Dedicated append-only consent ledger.
  • Authorized connectors for calendar, email, and documents — only behind the same explicit-approval pattern already used throughout Sentinel Node.
  • Real veteran and ecosystem data import (behind onboarding consent flows, not synthetic defaults).
Technical and governance pattern: Veteran Node follows Pocono AI's broader Sentinel Node pattern — local and imported data, role-specific situation picture, next-best-action draft, Governance Receipt, human review, consent-gated handoff, and auditable outcome. The product language throughout is deliberate: opportunity radar, trusted handoff, possible match, worth human review — never determined, approved, or endorsed.
