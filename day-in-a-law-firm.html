For Attorneys · Workflow Narrative

A day inside a small law firm using local AI.

This is what governed AI actually looks like inside a four-attorney general practice — from the first MyChart-style intake call at 8:14 AM to the moment the managing partner finally closes her laptop at 9:43 PM. No replacement narrative. No autonomous agents drafting briefs. Just retrieval, citation, attorney review, and an audit chain that holds up.

Note on this narrative. Sarah Mendez, David Walsh, and the staff of Mendez & Walsh do not exist. The firm, the matters, and the timestamps are fictitious composites assembled from interviews with practicing attorneys at small general-practice firms in Pennsylvania and New Jersey. The operational details — the retrieval moments, the review gates, the audit-chain behavior — reflect how Sentinel Node actually works. This page is meant to show, not sell.

Mendez & Walsh, PLLC

A four-attorney general practice in Stroudsburg, Pennsylvania. Founded eleven years ago when Sarah Mendez and David Walsh left a regional defense litigation shop. They take the work the bigger firms in Allentown won't drive an hour for. Most clients find them through referral.

Attorneys4 (2 partners, 2 associates)
Support staff1 paralegal · 1 legal assistant · 1 bookkeeper (PT)
Practice mixEstate planning · PI · Family · Civil litigation
Open matters~140 active · ~60 in active drafting

Managing Partner

Sarah Mendez

Estate planning and civil. 47. Still answers email at 11 PM.

Partner

David Walsh

Personal injury. Two kids in high school. Drives a 2014 Subaru.

Associate

Priya Krishnan

Family law. Third year out of Temple. Carries the custody docket.

Paralegal

Beth O'Connell

Has been at the firm since 2017. Knows where every PDF lives.

One day. Five attorneys. One Sentinel Node.

Tuesday, March 4. Each timeline entry is what actually happens. Each governance tag shows what the system enforced.

7:38
7:38 AM · Before Anyone Else Is In
Sarah arrives. The Hartman estate file is open on her desk where she left it Friday.

She has thirty-six minutes before the 8:15 intake call. The Hartman trust amendment needs a final pass before the client signs Thursday. She opens Sentinel Node and asks: "Summarize the changes between version 4 and version 6 of the Hartman trust amendment. Flag anything in version 6 that isn't supported by Mr. Hartman's signed instructions."

Sentinel Node · Retrieval · Local
MatterHartman, R. — Trust Amendment (E-2024-188)
Documentstrust_v4_draft.docx · trust_v6_draft.docx · client_instructions_signed.pdf
ScopeBounded to E-2024-188 folder · No external retrieval

Draft summary returns in 11 seconds. Each change is annotated with the source paragraph from the signed instructions. Two changes in v6 have no supporting instruction — they're flagged with ⚠ UNSUPPORTED rather than silently included.

She catches it. Provision 4(b) in v6 was added by mistake — it's from another client's template. Without the flag she would have spotted it in review, probably. Probably is a word she doesn't want to use about a trust document.

8:14
8:14 AM · First Call
Marcus picks up the intake line. A potential client is in tears.

Her husband was killed by a delivery truck on Route 209 last Friday. She's been told by three different people she has a case. She's been told by two different people she doesn't. Marcus opens the intake worksheet on his screen and starts asking the questions in order.

Sentinel Node listens to nothing. There is no microphone, no transcription. Marcus types what he needs into the intake form. When he marks the matter as a potential wrongful death, the system surfaces the firm's intake checklist for that matter type — twelve fields, the same twelve fields David built five years ago, retrieved from the firm's own knowledge base.

Intake · Wrongful Death Worksheet · Draft

Marcus completes seven of the twelve fields during the call. The other five — police report number, decedent's most recent W-2, names of witnesses — get marked PENDING. The matter is created with status Inquiry · Conflict check required. It will not appear in active workflows until David approves it.

What used to happen

Marcus would write notes on a legal pad. He'd remember to send them to David. Half the time the witnesses' names lived only in his memory until Wednesday.

What happens now

The worksheet exists, the gaps are visible, and the inquiry is in David's queue with a timestamp. Nothing moves to active without David signing it in.

9:22
9:22 AM · The Conflict Check
David runs the conflict check before he even pours coffee.

He asks Sentinel Node: "Have we ever represented anyone connected to McAllister Delivery Services, Carmen Reyes, or Edward Reyes in any matter at this firm?"

Conflict Check · Bounded Retrieval · Local
Searched11 years of matter records · 2,847 closed files · 140 active
Match1 result — Carmen Reyes appeared as a witness in PI-2019-31 (Cordero v. Brookfield Transport)
MatchNo results for McAllister Delivery Services or Edward Reyes

The witness appearance is flagged NO CONFLICT by the system — witness ≠ client — but David sees the citation and clicks through to the original 2019 file to confirm. Eleven seconds. He signs off the conflict check and the inquiry moves to active.

The thing he is most afraid of as a managing partner is the conflict he didn't catch. Not the malpractice case — the bar complaint. He has a real-time, citation-backed answer in less than a minute and a permanent audit record of having done the check.

10:48
10:48 AM · Custody Hearing Prep
Priya needs every prior order in the Donnelly custody matter. She has 47 minutes before she leaves for Monroe County.

There are nine prior orders in this case going back to 2021. Two are scanned, one is handwritten by the judge. Priya asks: "Surface every prior order in Donnelly v. Donnelly. For each, give me: date entered, judge, the operative custody schedule, and any provisions about religious upbringing or relocation."

Family Law · Document Retrieval · Citation-Bound

Nine orders return in a structured table. Eight have parsed citations. The ninth — the 2022 handwritten interim order — returns with OCR LOW CONFIDENCE and shows the scanned image inline. The system does not pretend to know what the judge wrote. It shows Priya the picture and asks her to read it herself.

Priya reads the handwritten paragraph. It's about supervised visitation during the father's substance abuse treatment — a provision that's no longer in effect but which opposing counsel may try to reference. She knows it now, going into court. Before, she would have found out at the bench.

She doesn't ask the AI to write her argument. She wouldn't. The argument is hers. What she asked the AI to do is retrieve, and that's what it did.

12:30
12:30 PM · Lunch (sort of)
Beth eats a salad at her desk and processes the morning's mail.

Forty-one pieces. Three are settlement offers. Two are subpoenas. Eleven are routine court notices. The rest is junk and bar association mailers. Sentinel Node scans the inbound stack as PDFs and proposes a routing for each: which matter, which attorney, what category. Beth confirms or corrects each one. The system is right on 38 of 41.

The three it got wrong include a settlement offer that came in on the wrong letterhead — a paralegal at opposing counsel's office used a generic template, and Sentinel Node couldn't tell which matter it belonged to. Beth knew immediately. Beth has been at the firm since 2017.

Mail Routing · Human Confirmation Required

No piece of correspondence is filed to a matter without a person clicking yes. The system suggests. The person decides. Every routing decision is stamped with the routing agent's name and a UTC timestamp.

2:17
2:17 PM · The Brief That Has To Be Filed Friday
Sarah opens the Halverson breach-of-contract matter. There are 1,400 pages of discovery in the firm's evidence index.

She asks: "For the breach claim under Count II, find every document that references the modified delivery schedule between October 12 and November 30. For each, give me the document name, the date, the speaker, the relevant quoted language, and a citation back to the file."

Civil Litigation · Evidence Retrieval · Sealed Index

Twenty-three documents return. Each one has a quoted excerpt and a clickable citation back to the original Bates-stamped page. Sarah reviews each citation against the underlying document.

Two of the AI's quoted excerpts have a subtle phrasing difference from the original. Not a hallucination — a paraphrase. The system shows both side by side and asks her which version she wants in her draft. She picks the verbatim. CITATION VERIFIED

She drafts the brief herself, in her own voice, citing the documents the system surfaced. The AI did not write her argument. It surfaced the evidence. Sarah is the lawyer; she's still doing what lawyers do.

By 5:30 PM the draft is in David's queue for partner review. Before Sentinel Node, this same evidence retrieval phase would have taken her — or Beth, depending on availability — a full Saturday.

3:48
3:48 PM · Where The System Refuses
A new associate at another firm calls Priya for an unrelated case. He's heard about Pocono AI and wants to know what it can do.

Priya, half listening, asks Sentinel Node on her end: "Draft a custody modification motion for me arguing the mother's relocation to Maryland materially affects the existing schedule."

System Response · Refusal With Reason

DECLINED. This request requires drafting a litigation motion that would advance a party's position in an active matter. Sentinel Node is configured to surface evidence and retrieve precedent, not to compose advocacy. The firm's governance policy (uploaded by D. Walsh, 2024-11-08) excludes AI-drafted motions, briefs, and pleadings from output scope. POLICY ENFORCED

Suggested alternatives: surface PA case law on relocation as material change, retrieve prior custody modification orders from firm precedent, generate an evidence summary table for attorney review.

Priya isn't surprised. She knew the system was going to say no. She wanted the associate on the phone to hear it. He asks how the policy is set. Priya tells him: "David wrote it. He decides what we let it do. He can change it. The system enforces what he decided."

6:50
6:50 PM · David's End-Of-Day Queue
David sits down to review the day. Eleven items in his queue.

Each item is an AI-assisted output that needs an attorney sign-off before it leaves the building. The Hartman trust summary (Sarah's morning), the new wrongful death intake (Marcus's call), three contract clauses Priya drafted for a small business client, the Halverson evidence summary (Sarah's afternoon), Beth's mail routing log, and four estate planning checklists pre-populated for tomorrow's appointments.

Partner Review Queue · 11 Items · D. Walsh
Approve9 items — clean, evidence cited, no flags
Send Back1 item — Halverson Count III citations need broader scope
Discuss1 item — wrongful death intake; David wants to call client tomorrow

Each approval writes a sealed audit envelope: who reviewed, when, on what version, with what result. Even the items sent back are recorded. Especially those. The audit chain doesn't care about the outcome; it cares about the trail.

9:43
9:43 PM · Sarah Closes Her Laptop
She got home at 7:10 PM, ate dinner with her teenager, came back to the home office at 8:20.

She reviewed the Halverson draft one more time, made a small edit to Count II, and put it back in David's queue. She read Priya's family-law brief for tomorrow. She read tomorrow's calendar.

Before, this same review pass would have taken her until 11:30 — searching her sent folder for the right version, switching between Outlook and the file share, second-guessing whether she had the latest. Tonight, every document was where Sentinel Node put it, every citation was already verified against the source, and every prior review by every attorney was visible in the audit trail.

She doesn't feel rested. She feels less behind.

What the day produced.

By the time the office locks at 9:48 PM, the day's audit chain contains 47 sealed transactions across 12 matters. Each transaction records: the prompt, the documents retrieved, the AI's draft output, the reviewing attorney, the review decision, the final version, and a cryptographic hash linking the sealed envelope to the previous one in the chain.

If the bar disciplinary board, opposing counsel, or a malpractice carrier ever asks how a particular output was produced, the answer is a single hash lookup:

env_2026-03-04_18:34:12Z_a7f3e1b8c9d4...

The record is local. The record is reviewable today, tamper-evident next. The record is the firm's, not the vendor's.

What the AI never did.

An honest account of where governance held the line.

Things AI marketing promises

Autonomous brief drafting. End-to-end client intake. AI-generated motions. AI-led negotiation. AI replacing the paralegal. AI replacing the associate. AI replacing the partner.

What Sentinel Node actually did

Retrieved evidence. Surfaced citations. Flagged uncertainty. Refused out-of-scope requests. Wrote audit envelopes. Did the document work that drowns small firms — and stopped at the line where an attorney's judgment belongs.

Sarah did not delegate her judgment. Priya did not delegate her advocacy. David did not delegate his conflict check. Beth did not delegate her instinct for what mail belongs where. What they delegated was the hours of retrieval, the paragraph-by-paragraph comparison, the citation-checking, the routing — the operational work that small-firm attorneys carry on top of being lawyers.
