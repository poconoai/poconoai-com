Workflow Proof  ·  Cardiology

Cardiology risk does not only live in the diagnosis. It lives in the inbox.

A single cardiology day can generate abnormal labs, echo reports, Holter/Zio findings, portal messages, clearance requests, incidental findings, and follow-up obligations. Sentinel Node helps practices retrieve context, route work, document decisions, and close the loop — locally.

Workflow Comparison

The same day, without and with Sentinel Node

Each event below is a real category of cardiology inbox obligation. The columns show the operational difference between a practice that runs the workflow manually and a practice that uses a local, human-reviewed Sentinel review layer.

7:38 AM

Inbox starts overloaded

Without Sentinel Node
  • Cardiologist opens EHR before first patient and sees a pile of unsorted items
  • Routine, abnormal, and urgent items are intermixed with no triage layer
  • Mental triage required just to decide where to start
  • Items deferred to lunch or after hours by default
  • Documentation scattered across notes, calls, messages, and EHR clicks
With Sentinel Node
  • Items pre-classified into workflow categories (routine, abnormal, monitor, imaging, message, clearance)
  • Open-loop conditions flagged before clinician opens the chart
  • Items already assigned to suggested owners pending clinician confirmation
  • Closure status visible on each item
  • Audit trail preserved from intake onward

8:12 AM

High-risk lab needs context

Without Sentinel Node
  • Potassium and creatinine return; heart failure patient on potassium-affecting therapy
  • Clinician hunts through chart for trend, prior labs, medication changes, symptoms
  • Owner of the patient call is unclear — cardiologist, APP, or nurse?
  • Repeat-lab need decided in isolation, often without staff task generated
  • Follow-up may remain open after the result is “reviewed”
With Sentinel Node
  • Lab classified as medication-safety item; trend retrieved automatically
  • Prior potassium, creatinine, EGFR, and recent medication changes assembled in a packet
  • Open loops flagged: no repeat lab, no medication review note, no patient contact
  • Suggested owner: cardiologist review → staff call task drafted
  • Patient call script drafted for clinician sign-off; closure status tracked

9:26 AM

Echo result creates follow-up obligation

Without Sentinel Node
  • EF drop from ~50% to ~35% — clinically meaningful, but the report alone does not close the loop
  • Clinician must check prior echo, current meds, last visit, and follow-up status manually
  • Patient notification may not get documented even if the call is made
  • Medication-optimization plan may sit in a note without a scheduled visit
  • Follow-up appointment status is invisible from inside the report itself
With Sentinel Node
  • Echo classified as abnormal result requiring follow-up
  • EF trend, last cardiology note, current medications, and appointment status retrieved
  • Open loops flagged: no follow-up visit scheduled, no documented patient notification
  • Patient call and medication-review staff tasks drafted for clinician sign-off
  • Closure stays “open” until appointment scheduled and notification documented

10:41 AM

Incidental finding creates ownership ambiguity

Without Sentinel Node
  • Cardiac CT includes an incidental pulmonary nodule with a follow-up recommendation
  • Cardiology received the study; the finding is non-cardiac
  • PCP may or may not have been copied; nobody is sure who owns follow-up
  • Patient may not understand or remember the recommendation
  • The finding can disappear into the chart with no documented owner
With Sentinel Node
  • Classified as incidental non-cardiac finding — one of the highest-risk inbox categories
  • Ordering clinician, PCP copy status, and patient notification status retrieved
  • Owner-assignment queue flags the item until ownership is explicitly transferred or accepted
  • PCP notification packet and patient communication drafted for clinician review
  • Tracking date set; closure requires documented ownership transfer

12:18 PM

Holter/Zio report requires context-dependent review

Without Sentinel Node
  • Monitor report returns showing runs of nonsustained VT
  • Clinician must reconstruct symptoms, prior rhythm history, last EF, structural heart disease, beta blocker use
  • Anticoagulation context may need to be retrieved manually
  • Patient notification status not visible from the report view
  • Final disposition may be reviewed but not fully closed
With Sentinel Node
  • Classified as a rhythm report requiring clinician review — the pilot’s anchor case
  • Reason ordered, prior EKG, last echo and EF, beta blocker / antiarrhythmic use, anticoagulation status, and upcoming appointment retrieved
  • Open loops flagged: no clinician review yet, no patient notification, no follow-up visit, no documented plan
  • Routing packet, staff task, and closure checklist drafted for review — nothing acted on autonomously
  • Closure remains open until patient notification and follow-up plan are documented or explicitly closed

2:07 PM

Portal message mentions AFib symptoms

Without Sentinel Node
  • Patient sends a portal message: “My watch says I’m in afib again, I feel weird and a little short of breath”
  • Message may be routed like a routine portal item
  • Symptom context, anticoagulation status, recent test results not surfaced automatically
  • Triage owner unclear; escalation depends on who happens to see it
  • Message may age without a documented response
With Sentinel Node
  • Classified as a symptom-bearing portal message — not routine
  • Relevant diagnoses, anticoagulation status, recent test results, and prior calls retrieved
  • Routed to RN triage queue under practice-defined escalation rules; cardiologist notified if criteria met
  • Sentinel does not message the patient directly — it prepares context for human triage
  • Closure remains open until documented response and disposition

4:36 PM

Clearance request involves anticoagulation

Without Sentinel Node
  • Dental or surgical office asks whether a patient can stop Eliquis before a procedure
  • Appears administrative but is not purely administrative
  • Clinician must reconstruct indication, stroke risk, procedure bleeding risk, timing, recent symptoms
  • Response may be completed without documenting the clinical basis
  • External-office follow-up status may not be tracked
With Sentinel Node
  • Classified as a clearance request requiring clinician review — not auto-answered
  • Indication for anticoagulation, stroke and bleeding risk context, recent symptoms, and procedure timing retrieved
  • Clearance response packet drafted for clinician review with documented clinical basis
  • External-office reply tracked through closure
  • Audit trail preserved: source request, retrieved evidence, clinician decision, sent response, timestamps

6:18 PM

The second shift begins

Without Sentinel Node
  • Clinic ends; inbox remains
  • Cardiologist reviews reports, signs refills, looks up prior studies, routes tasks after hours
  • EHR records clicks but does not necessarily prove closure
  • Burnout, risk, and liability compound in fragmented work
  • Family time absorbs the cost
With Sentinel Node
  • Day-end review queue is shorter because context retrieval and routing happened earlier
  • Open-loop list is visible: still-open obligations are explicit, not implicit
  • Items remaining are clinician-action items, not chart-hunting tasks
  • Audit trail captures what was reviewed, when, by whom, with what evidence, with what disposition
  • After-hours work shifts from reconstruction to review

Closure Is The Standard

The difference between reviewed and closed is where risk lives.

The EHR may show that a result was opened. The safer question is whether the clinically meaningful obligation was assigned, reviewed, communicated, followed up, documented, and closed.

Why Local Matters

Sentinel Node is local workflow infrastructure, not autonomous medicine

Cardiology inbox data is among the most sensitive in a practice — diagnoses, medications, symptoms, anticoagulation decisions, imaging findings, patient messages. The most sensitive workflow in the practice should not require sending patient context to an external AI service by default.

Local audit trail

Role-based queues

Sentinel Node does not diagnose, does not autonomously message patients, does not autonomously clear patients for surgery, does not autonomously change medications, and does not determine emergency urgency on its own. It supports human-reviewed workflow closure.
