For Paralegals · Legal Assistants · Support Staff

Less hunting. More working.

The institutional knowledge a paralegal carries is what holds a small law firm together. Sentinel Node is not built to replace that knowledge — it's built to remove the operational chaos that buries it under file shares, Outlook chains, and PDF stacks.

The promise to support staff: Your job involves judgment. It also involves twenty hours a week of retrieval, sorting, indexing, and chasing things down. Sentinel Node is designed to take the second part — not the first. The professional oversight remains yours.
01 · PARALEGAL

Paralegal

The institutional memory of the firm.

The work that drowns you

  • Hunting through six years of Outlook chains for the email where opposing counsel confirmed a deadline extension.
  • Building chronologies from scratch — pulling dates from depositions, exhibits, contract clauses, and email threads.
  • Organizing exhibits for trial: hundreds of documents that have to be Bates-stamped, numbered, indexed, and cross-referenced.
  • Discovery indexing across multiple productions — vendor file, opposing counsel file, your own ESI capture.
  • Tracking down which version of a contract is the operative one when the deal closed three years ago.

What Sentinel Node does for you

  • Bounded search across firm matter records. One query surfaces the email, the deposition excerpt, and the contract clause together — with citations to where each came from.
  • Chronology assembly assistance. The system extracts dated references from documents you upload, structures them into a timeline, and shows you the source paragraph for each entry. You verify and edit; the system does the assembly.
  • Exhibit organization with Bates-stamped citations. The system produces a candidate exhibit index with citations to original documents. You confirm, reorder, and finalize.
  • Duplicate detection. When the same document appears in three productions with different file names, the system flags the duplicates and shows the differences side by side.
⊘ The system will not

Decide which exhibits are relevant. Make legal determinations about admissibility. Choose what gets included in a brief. Those are attorney decisions. The system surfaces; the attorney decides; you organize the result.

02 · LEGAL ASSISTANT

Legal Assistant

The front line of the firm.

The work that drowns you

  • Sorting inbound mail and email across forty-plus matters, routing each piece to the right attorney and the right file.
  • Filling intake worksheets while a client is on the phone in crisis — and trying to remember the five fields you didn't get to.
  • Scheduling around four attorneys' competing calendars while keeping the bookkeeping current.
  • Drafting routine correspondence — letters confirming receipt of documents, hearing reminders, status updates to clients.
  • Maintaining the firm's contact database across Outlook, the case management system, and the bookkeeper's spreadsheet.

What Sentinel Node does for you

  • Inbound mail routing proposals. The system reads scanned correspondence and proposes a routing — matter, attorney, category. You confirm or correct. Every routing decision is logged in your name.
  • Intake worksheets with PENDING-field tracking. The fields you couldn't fill during the call stay visible as PENDING until completed. The system doesn't fabricate the missing fields — it shows you what's missing.
  • Routine correspondence drafting from firm templates. The system populates standard letters from your firm's own template library, with the client's name, matter number, and dates pulled from the matter record. Attorney signs off before anything goes out.
  • Cross-system contact deduplication. Identifies likely duplicate contacts across the firm's systems and proposes consolidations. You decide which to merge.
⊘ The system will not

Send correspondence on its own. File anything to a court. Make scheduling commitments to clients. Confirm receipt of legal service. Anything that leaves the firm requires a person clicking yes.

03 · INTAKE

Intake Coordinator

The first impression. Often the hardest call.

The work that drowns you

  • Taking intake calls from clients in crisis — wrongful death, custody, criminal — while staying composed enough to capture twelve required fields.
  • Running conflict checks across years of closed files before you can even tell the caller whether the firm can help.
  • Tracking inquiries that came in but never got to the partner's queue.
  • Reconstructing intake details on Wednesday when the partner asks about a Monday morning call.
  • Capturing referral source data accurately, even when the caller can barely speak.

What Sentinel Node does for you

  • Matter-type-specific intake worksheets. When you mark the call as a wrongful death, the system surfaces the firm's twelve-field intake form for that matter type. Estate planning, personal injury, family law — each has its own.
  • Real-time conflict-check while you talk. As you type names into the worksheet, the system runs a bounded conflict check against firm records and shows results in seconds — so you know whether to keep going or break the news gently.
  • Pending inquiry queue. Every intake call generates an inquiry record with status PENDING ATTORNEY REVIEW. Nothing moves to active without a partner's signoff. Nothing falls through to Wednesday.
  • Capture-as-you-go, not after. The worksheet exists during the call. Notes get structured immediately. The five fields you couldn't fill stay visible as PENDING.
⊘ The system will not

Listen to your call. Transcribe what the client says. Record audio of any kind. The intake worksheet is what you type, when you type it. Nothing more.

04 · LITIGATION SUPPORT

Litigation Support Staff

Where Saturdays go to disappear.

The work that drowns you

  • Processing discovery productions — sometimes thousands of pages at a time — into searchable, citable form.
  • Preparing deposition outlines: pulling every prior statement a witness has made on a given topic.
  • Summarizing deposition transcripts after the fact for the attorney who took the deposition.
  • Building privilege logs while keeping the underlying privileged documents from contamination.
  • Coordinating exhibit preparation for trial — the night before, every time.

What Sentinel Node does for you

  • Bates-stamped retrieval across the sealed evidence index. One query surfaces every document in a production that references a topic, date range, or person — each excerpt clickable to the original Bates page.
  • Deposition prep retrieval. The system pulls every prior statement a witness has made across the firm's deposition transcript library on a given topic. Verbatim, with transcript page citations.
  • Transcript summarization with citation back to line numbers. The system produces a candidate summary of a deposition. Every assertion in the summary cites back to the transcript page and line. You verify.
  • Privilege boundary enforcement. Documents marked privileged stay in a sealed scope. The system enforces this at the retrieval layer — privileged documents don't appear in non-privileged queries.
⊘ The system will not

Make privilege determinations. Decide what's responsive to a discovery request. Compose deposition questions. Draft motions. Strategic decisions in litigation belong to the attorney.

05 · OFFICE ADMIN

Office Administrator

The glue. Usually invisible. Always essential.

The work that drowns you

  • Reconciling time entries across the four attorneys for monthly billing — chasing missing entries, correcting typos, allocating to the right matter.
  • Coordinating CLE compliance, malpractice insurance renewals, vendor contracts, and IT.
  • Managing the firm's document retention schedule and storage costs across paper, scanned, and digital files.
  • Onboarding new staff while keeping the existing systems running.
  • Producing reports for the partners: realization rates, matter aging, client concentration.

What Sentinel Node does for you

  • Cross-matter time-entry validation. The system flags time entries that don't match active matter records, point to closed matters, or appear to be misallocated. You decide what to correct.
  • Document retention surfacing. Identifies matters and documents approaching retention thresholds per the firm's retention policy. Proposes flags; you and the partner decide what to keep, archive, or destroy.
  • Standard report generation from firm data. Realization rate by matter type, aging report, client concentration — produced from the firm's own records, locally, without exporting to a vendor.
  • Onboarding asset surfacing. When a new staff member joins, the system can surface the firm's policies, templates, and reference documents relevant to their role — from the firm's own knowledge base.
⊘ The system will not

Send invoices. Initiate payments. Make billing-rate decisions. Communicate with insurance carriers. Issue 1099s. Anything financial or fiduciary requires a person signing off.

What Beth knows.

In the Day In A Small Law Firm narrative, Beth O'Connell — the paralegal at the fictitious Mendez & Walsh — has been at the firm since 2017. She knows where every PDF lives. She knows which judge takes a hard line on continuances. She knows which client always pays late but always pays. She knows that opposing counsel's paralegal uses a generic settlement-offer template that's easy to miss.

Sentinel Node was not designed to replace what Beth knows. It was designed to remove the hours of retrieval, sorting, and routing that bury what Beth knows under operational chaos. The institutional knowledge stays with the people who built it. The administrative weight comes off.

That is the promise to your support staff. Less hunting. More working. The judgment remains theirs.
