Safe Use

What Sentinel Node Is and Is Not

Every customer training session begins here. Before any workflow, every user must understand the fundamental boundaries.

What Sentinel Node does

  • Retrieves from approved, local, in-scope documents
  • Drafts structured packets from cited evidence
  • Flags missing or stale records
  • Routes every output to a human reviewer
  • Logs every step in a reviewable audit trail

What Sentinel Node does not do

  • Practice medicine or law
  • Submit, file, fax, prescribe, diagnose, approve, or deny
  • Make autonomous clinical or legal decisions
  • Replace professional judgment or sign-off
  • Guarantee approval, outcome, or reimbursement
Approved-source retrieval only. Sentinel Node only retrieves from the approved, in-scope document set defined for the pilot. It does not access the internet, cloud services, or records outside the defined scope. Retrieval is bounded, not open-ended.

Citation checking

Every draft cites its sources. Users must verify each citation against the actual source document before routing to a reviewer. “The system said it” is not a substitute for checking the source.

Human review required

No output is final until reviewed, edited, approved, and owned by the responsible professional. Staff do not approve outputs. The licensed clinician or attorney does.

Approved sources only

Users ask Sentinel Node to retrieve from the approved, defined source set. They do not ask it to invent, extrapolate, or supplement from memory or general knowledge.

Module 1

Patient Packet / Pre-Visit Packet

Help staff prepare a cited, reviewable packet before a patient visit, referral, care conference, or follow-up.

Current pain

Staff search across EHR exports, scanned PDFs, labs, imaging, outside records, prior notes, medication history, and old messages. The information may exist — but it is scattered and hard to trust quickly under time pressure.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user selects the approved patient record set and asks Sentinel Node to prepare a pre-visit packet. The system retrieves from local approved sources, drafts a structured packet, and cites the source material behind each important claim.

Packet may include

Reason for visit · Relevant diagnoses · Current medication context

Recent labs and imaging · Prior treatments · Specialist notes

Unresolved questions · Missing or stale documents · Source citations for review

Human review boundary. The packet is a draft. It is not a clinical note, order, diagnosis, or final medical judgment. The responsible clinician verifies sources, edits the packet, and owns the final version.

Training exercise

Give staff a synthetic patient chart. Produce a pre-visit packet, verify every citation, identify missing records, and escalate at least one uncertain item.

Pilot measures

  • Time to packet
  • Missing-document flags
  • Reviewer corrections
  • Unsupported claims caught
  • Reviewer confidence
  • Staff friction log
Module 2

Prior Authorization Support Packet

Help staff assemble a complete, cited prior authorization support packet from approved local records.

Current pain

Prior authorization work often fails because supporting evidence is scattered. Staff must find diagnoses, dates, failed therapies, medication trials, conservative treatment history, imaging, labs, specialist notes, and payer-specific criteria.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user asks Sentinel Node to prepare a prior authorization support packet. The system retrieves from local approved documents and drafts a structured packet for human review. The goal is first-submission completeness, not guaranteed approval.

Packet may include

Requested service, medication, imaging, or procedure · Diagnosis and relevant clinical history

Prior therapies tried and failed · Dates and source references · Supporting labs or specialist notes

Medical-necessity draft language · Missing-evidence checklist · Reviewer sign-off area

Human review boundary. Sentinel Node does not submit the prior authorization, communicate with payers, or guarantee approval. It prepares draft support material from local evidence for staff and clinician review only.

Training exercise

Use a synthetic prior authorization scenario. Assemble the packet, verify every citation, flag missing evidence, and route the draft to the responsible reviewer.

Pilot measures

  • Time per packet
  • First-submission completeness
  • Missing-evidence rate
  • Reviewer edits
  • Denial reason tracking
  • Staff friction log
Module 3

Denial Response / Appeal Support Packet

Help staff respond to a denial by organizing the denial reason, prior submission, local evidence, and missing documentation into a reviewable packet.

Current pain

When a denial arrives, staff often restart the process manually. The denial letter, payer rationale, original submission, chart evidence, and next-step options may be disconnected.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user provides the denial reason and approved local source set. Sentinel Node prepares a denial response support packet with cited evidence and a missing-evidence checklist — an appeal-ready packet for professional review.

Packet may include

Denial reason summary · Payer’s stated basis · Evidence already submitted

Evidence missing from the first submission · Clinical timeline · Draft appeal support language

Supporting citations · Reviewer checklist · Human decision documented

Human review boundary. Sentinel Node does not decide whether to appeal, does not practice medicine or law, and does not provide insurance advocacy. It organizes evidence so the responsible professional can decide what to do.

Training exercise

Give staff a synthetic denial letter. Build a denial response packet, identify what was missing from the first submission, verify sources, and send the draft to the reviewer.

Pilot measures

  • Time to appeal-ready packet
  • Missing-document identification
  • Reviewer edits
  • Appeal readiness score
  • Final human decision documented
Module 4

H&P / Document Chaos Support

Help clinicians and staff turn chaotic records into a source-grounded, reviewable summary without treating the AI output as a final note.

Current pain

Clinical records are often long, duplicated, stale, fragmented, or copied forward. H&Ps, consult notes, hospital records, outside PDFs, medication lists, and test results can hide the facts that matter.

Sentinel Node workflow

The user asks Sentinel Node to prepare a cited documentation support summary from approved local records. The system retrieves relevant source material and identifies what appears current, stale, missing, or conflicting.

Output may include

Key history elements · Relevant past medical history · Medication and allergy context

Prior procedures · Recent tests and results · Conflicting information flags

Missing information checklist · Source list for reviewer verification · “Do not rely without review” warning

Human review boundary. The output is not an H&P. It is not a signed note. The clinician owns the final documentation and must verify accuracy before any clinical use.

Training exercise

Use a synthetic chaotic chart with duplicate notes, stale medication history, and missing outside records. Identify what is reliable, what is stale, and what must be escalated.

Pilot measures

  • Time to usable summary
  • Unsupported claims caught
  • Stale records identified
  • Reviewer corrections
  • After-hours documentation friction
  • Staff confidence
Required Drill

Bad Output and Missing Evidence Drill

Every customer user must practice the bad-output workflow before going live. This drill is not optional.

Required workflow — no exceptions: Stop → Do not use → Identify the problem → Check the citation → Escalate to reviewer → Correct and rerun fresh → Review again → Log the issue.
1

Stop

Do not use the output. Do not forward, file, or act on it.

2

Identify the problem

What is missing, stale, conflicting, unsupported, or uncertain? Be specific.

3

Check the cited source

Does the citation support the claim? If not, that is the specific finding to escalate.

4

Escalate to the responsible reviewer

Notify the Responsible Reviewer and Practice Administrator.

5

Correct and rerun fresh

Correct the source set or prompt. Rerun only with fresh context. Full review of the new output before any use.

6

Log the issue

Record in the pilot friction log. Do not suppress it. Documented observations are how the system improves.

Reviewer Sign-Off

Role-Based Sign-Off

Every output has a required human sign-off before professional use.

Staff / pilot user

Prepares the request, checks citations, flags missing evidence, and routes the draft to the Responsible Reviewer. Does not approve final outputs.

Responsible Reviewer

The licensed professional (physician, attorney, or designated senior reviewer) who reads, edits, approves, or rejects the draft. Owns the final output.

Practice Administrator / Pilot Lead

Oversees the pilot, collects friction logs, tracks reviewer sign-off completion, and escalates systematic issues.

Pilot Metrics

What a Customer Pilot Measures

No pilot metric implies guaranteed clinical, legal, or reimbursement outcomes. All metrics are measured proof-of-value indicators in a bounded pilot environment.
  • Time to packet (baseline vs. Sentinel Node)
  • First-submission completeness rate
  • Missing-evidence found per packet
  • Unsupported claims caught before reviewer
  • Stale records identified
  • Citation accuracy score
  • Reviewer correction count per packet
  • Human sign-off completion rate
  • Final output used / not used (with reason)
  • Staff friction severity per session
  • Bad-output events logged
  • Staff confidence change (pre/post pilot)
