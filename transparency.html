Research Citation Transparency

Pocono AI publishes research statistics on EHR burden, prior authorization overhead, breach costs, and legal administrative load to inform product design decisions. The following policies govern how we handle those citations:

  • Source types are labeled. Peer-reviewed studies, government/federal data, recognized industry surveys (AMA, ABA, BLS, IBM, MGMA, KFF, Bloomberg Law, Clio), and vendor/trade sources are identified separately. A confidence tier (High / Medium / Low) is assigned per the citation audit table on research.html.
  • Derived figures are labeled. Any statistic that is a calculation or extrapolation from source data (rather than a directly published figure) is identified as “illustrative calculation.”
  • Conservative language. Where figures vary across sources, we favor the more conservative number. Where a claim cannot be directly supported by the cited source, we soften it to match what the source actually says.
  • No clinical or legal advice. Research summaries on this site are used to frame product design rationale. They are not legal, medical, or financial advice. Licensed professionals must make their own determinations.
  • Live-source verification flags. Items marked ⚠ in the citation audit table require live-source confirmation before being quoted in regulatory, clinical, or investor contexts. See research.html for the full table.

SEO Language Transparency (v347)

As of v347, the Pocono AI website uses more explicit buyer-facing search phrases across the homepage, physician pages, attorney pages, and new landing pages. This section explains what those phrases mean and what they do not mean.

What we added: Natural language integrations of phrases such as “HIPAA-aware local AI,” “local AI for medical practices,” “private AI for law firms,” “AI scribe alternative,” “on-premises legal AI,” “attorney-client privilege AI,” “private RAG,” “AI audit trail,” “tamper-evident logs,” “human-reviewed AI workflow,” and related terms. These are used in page text, title tags, and meta descriptions where contextually appropriate.

What these phrases mean:

  • “HIPAA-aware” / “HIPAA-sensitive” / “HIPAA-aligned” — These describe design intent. The Sentinel Node is designed so that patient data processed by the AI model does not leave the customer’s physical premises by default. This is an architectural feature, not a compliance certification. No vendor can certify HIPAA compliance on behalf of a covered entity. Each organization remains responsible for its own risk analysis, policies, BAA review, and compliance determinations.
  • Interactive scenario tools are educational. The site includes an interactive scenario tool (the Strategic AI What-If Scenario Engine). Its incident simulations, governance scores, and briefing panels are illustrative and for educational purposes only. They are not legal advice, compliance advice, or regulatory determinations, and they do not guarantee any outcome. Organizations should consult qualified legal and compliance counsel before making AI infrastructure decisions.
  • “Attorney-client privilege AI” / “confidential legal AI” — These describe the architecture’s local-custody design. Keeping documents on-premises reduces transmission vectors that could implicate privilege waiver. This is not a legal guarantee. Privilege determinations depend on jurisdiction, facts, and counsel review. Attorneys remain responsible for their own privilege analysis and professional obligations.
  • “AI scribe alternative” — Sentinel Node can assist with clinical documentation drafting. It is not a licensed medical scribe service, does not make clinical determinations, and does not replace professional clinical judgment. All AI-generated clinical drafts require physician review before use.
  • “AI audit trail” / “tamper-evident logs” — Sentinel Node uses reviewable audit logs stored on-device for 90 days by default. These are designed to support reviewability and auditability. They are not a substitute for legal hold procedures, formal records management, or regulatory reporting obligations.

What SEO language is not: The inclusion of search-optimized phrases in marketing copy does not constitute a compliance guarantee, product certification, legal opinion, or clinical validation. Prospective customers should evaluate their own regulatory environment with qualified legal and compliance counsel before deploying any AI system.
