Sentinel Node Deployment · Training

Clinical training built for how clinicians actually learn.

The training program assumes the trainee is busy, skeptical, and operating under real time pressure. It is short, role-specific, and focused on the verification habits that make AI-assisted output safe to act on. The goal is not to teach the technology. The goal is to teach the verification.

Two sessions, two audiences

The training is intentionally short and split by role. A physician’s verification workflow differs from a medical assistant’s; the former exercises clinical judgment, the latter exercises procedural diligence. Forcing one curriculum on both audiences wastes time on both sides.

Physician session · 60 minutes

  • How to phrase a clinical query so retrieval surfaces relevant chart entries
  • How to read a citation and verify it against the source line
  • When to accept an AI-drafted note, when to edit, when to reject
  • What the audit log captures and how it protects the clinician
  • Escalation protocol for clinical uncertainty

Outcome: the physician can use the Sentinel Node in a real patient encounter the same day, with verification habits in place.

Administrative staff session · 45 minutes

  • Role-based access boundaries: which document types are visible to which roles
  • How to use the prior-auth and scheduling workflows the system supports
  • What "attestation required" means and why it stops the workflow
  • How to flag a retrieved citation that appears incorrect
  • Privacy and HIPAA reminders specific to the local deployment posture

Outcome: administrative staff can process the workflows that don’t require clinician attestation, and know exactly when to escalate.

Verification habits we teach

The training is built around a small set of repeatable verification habits. Each one is short enough to fit in the gap between patients.

  • Source-first reading. Read the cited chart entry before reading the AI’s synthesis of it. The synthesis is a hypothesis; the chart is the truth.
  • Citation density check. If a clinical claim lacks a citation, treat it as a draft until you supply one.
  • Edit, don’t accept silently. An edited draft signals to the audit log that the clinician engaged with the content. A silently-accepted draft signals less.
  • Escalate uncertainty. If the retrieval seems incomplete or the chart contradicts itself, route to a colleague rather than commit to the AI’s synthesis.

Why the training is short

Long training programs lose clinicians in the first hour. The Sentinel Node is designed to be operable with 45–60 minutes of role-specific instruction because the system itself enforces the structure: every output cites its sources, every action requires explicit attestation, every step is logged. The training teaches recognition, not procedure.

Follow-up reinforcement happens during the Phase 4 validation period of the pilot deployment, where a Pocono AI engineer is on-call for the first two weeks to answer real-workflow questions as they arise.

What the training does not do

  • It does not teach prompt engineering. The workflows are bounded by retrieval scope; prompt-craft is not the value.
  • It does not teach AI internals. Clinicians do not need to understand model architecture to verify clinical citations.
  • It does not certify competence. There is no certificate. The audit log is the record of how the system was used in practice.
  • It does not replace clinical judgment training. AI verification is a layer on top of clinical training, not a substitute for it.

For Clinicians: The AI Does Not Own the Note

Sentinel Node may help assemble source-grounded context, but it does not own the clinical judgment. The clinician remains responsible for the final note, final plan, final order, final referral, final prior authorization support language, and final patient communication.

The system’s role is to make the evidence visible before the human decision is made — not to make the decision, not to replace verification, and not to produce a signed clinical document.

What Sentinel Node assists with

  • Surface evidence before the visit
  • Assemble prior auth support packets
  • Organize denial response evidence
  • Flag missing or conflicting records
  • Cite sources for reviewer verification

What Sentinel Node does not do

  • Write or sign a final H&P
  • Make clinical decisions
  • Submit prior authorizations
  • Communicate with payers
  • Replace physician judgment
