Sentinel Node Measured operational impact

A local AI appliance has to earn its place operationally — not just philosophically.

This page translates Sentinel Node into the language practice managers, CFOs, and managing partners need: what work changes, what burden is measured, and what evidence a paid POV should produce before anyone claims ROI.

Book a deployment conversationSee the pilot evidence framework
Time burdenMeasure staff and professional time spent locating records, assembling packets, drafting first-pass responses, and reconciling source evidence.
Risk burdenTrack where unsupported drafts, missing citations, policy drift, or unclear review status create liability exposure.
Expansion burdenUse pilot evidence to decide whether a workflow deserves expansion, refinement, or rejection before larger spend.

The five-part impact model

The goal is not inflated savings claims. The goal is a conservative before-and-after record that leadership can defend.

1

Baseline

Document current workflow time, handoffs, rework, and unresolved friction before Sentinel Node is introduced.

2

Instrument

Define what the appliance logs: retrieval source, citation use, review state, reviewer action, and final disposition.

3

Compare

Measure whether first-pass assembly, evidence lookup, and review preparation improve against the baseline.

4

Validate

Have humans verify whether outputs were usable, cited, complete, and safe enough for draft-stage workflow support.

5

Decide

Close the POV with continue, modify, expand, or stop — based on evidence rather than hype.

Conservative claim standard
Pocono AI should not promise guaranteed savings before the pilot. It should promise a disciplined measurement process that can prove whether the appliance reduces burden in a specific environment.
