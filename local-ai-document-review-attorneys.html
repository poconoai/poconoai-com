Local AI Document Review That Keeps the File On Your Hardware

Document review is where AI can save a small firm the most time — and where sending the file to the cloud is the least acceptable. Sentinel Node runs first-pass document review on hardware inside your firm: it surfaces and summarizes candidate documents with citations, flags what it is unsure about, and logs every step for attorney review.

Language note: Sentinel Node assists with first-pass document review. It is not a substitute for attorney review, does not render legal conclusions, and does not determine privilege or responsiveness on its own. “Local” and “no cloud” describe the architecture. Attorney review remains the control point on every output.

Who this is for

  • Attorneys and paralegals at small firms handling discovery and document-heavy matters.
  • Firms that cannot route privileged production sets through a cloud vendor.
  • Practices that want review speed without giving up custody of the file.

The problem

A cloud document-review tool only works by ingesting your documents into a vendor's system. For a law firm, those documents are privileged and confidential, and the firm loses physical custody the moment they are uploaded. The firm is then trusting a vendor's controls over its clients' most sensitive material — and accepting that the file now lives inside a third party's breach surface.

How Pocono AI approaches it

Sentinel Node performs first-pass review locally. A production set is loaded onto the appliance, where the model retrieves and ranks documents responsive to a defined issue, drafts short summaries that cite the source document, and flags low-confidence matches for attorney attention. Nothing is transmitted off the firm's network, and every query and review action is recorded in the matter's local audit trail.

A realistic workflow: issue-based first-pass review

  1. A paralegal loads a production set for one matter and defines the issue to review for.
  2. Sentinel Node retrieves and ranks candidate documents, drafting a short cited summary of why each may be relevant.
  3. Documents the model is unsure about are flagged for human attention rather than silently included or excluded.
  4. The attorney reviews the shortlist; the queries, retrieved documents, and review decisions are written to the matter's audit trail. The file never left the firm.

Why local document review matters

Document review puts a firm's most sensitive material — entire privileged production sets — into the AI pipeline. Doing that in the cloud means handing custody to a vendor. A local appliance keeps the file inside the firm: the documents never leave hardware the firm controls, the deployment can be air-gapped, and the firm's exposure stays within its own walls. Review gets faster without the file getting further away.

Human review and the audit trail

Sentinel Node returns AI output as a draft, never a decision. A qualified person reviews before anything is relied on, and the language model is never called directly — every interaction passes through an Audit Proxy that records the query, the retrieved sources, the output, and the human sign-off into a reviewable local audit log. That keeps the record reviewable and in your custody rather than a vendor cloud.

What Pocono AI does not claim

  • We do not claim the AI determines privilege or responsiveness — it surfaces candidates for attorney judgment.
  • We do not claim it replaces attorney review; it produces a reviewable, cited shortlist.
  • We do not guarantee it catches every relevant document — it is a first-pass aid, and the attorney remains responsible.

Frequently asked questions

Does the production set go to the cloud?

No. Documents are loaded onto the local appliance and review runs there. No part of the production set is routed to external endpoints.

Does the AI decide privilege or responsiveness?

No. It surfaces and summarizes candidates with citations and flags uncertainty. Privilege and responsiveness determinations remain attorney judgments.

How does it cite its work?

Each summary references the specific source document it drew from, so an attorney can verify the basis for inclusion rather than trusting an unsourced conclusion.

What happens to documents the AI is unsure about?

They are flagged for human attention. The system is built to escalate uncertainty rather than silently include or drop a document.

Is there a record of the review?

Yes. Queries, retrieved documents, and review decisions are written to the matter's local audit trail, held by the firm.
