The Paradox of Automated Abandonment

A verified analysis of AI-driven healthcare malpractice liability. Four enforcement actions. Three jurisdictions. Thirty days. A defensible damage-modeling framework under Pennsylvania law. No point predictions. Verified citations only.

Executive Summary

Cloud-native large language models in healthcare and law have transitioned from productivity tools to liability triggers. Between April and May 2026, four enforcement actions across three U.S. jurisdictions established a pattern that reframes AI hallucinations from software defects into professional failures.

This paper expands the April 2026 “Malpractice Trap” thesis with verified docket citations. It does not predict a specific verdict on a specific date. It presents a defensible damage-modeling framework demonstrating that an AI-driven medical abandonment case combining catastrophic injury with willful misconduct sits on a verdict trajectory of low-to-mid eight figures under existing Pennsylvania law.

The defensible architectural response is not better cloud prompts. It is local custody of model weights, source-grounded retrieval inference, and provable human-in-the-loop review.

Part I — The Verified Judicial Climate (April–May 2026)

Four cases over thirty days, in three different jurisdictions, with three different theories of liability. The pattern matters more than any single case.

Oregon — D. Or. — April 4, 2026

Brigandi & Murphy — $110,000 Sanction — Largest AI Citation Penalty in U.S. History

U.S. Magistrate Judge Mark D. Clarke imposed $110,000 in combined fines and attorneys’ fees against two attorneys for filing three briefs containing 15 fabricated AI case citations and 8 fabricated quotations. The underlying vineyard lawsuit was dismissed with prejudice. The court characterized the conduct as a deliberate cover-up after the AI errors were flagged by opposing counsel.

“In the quickly expanding universe of cases involving sanctions for the misuse of artificial intelligence, this case is a notorious outlier in both degree and volume.”
— U.S. Mag. J. Mark D. Clarke, Op. of Apr. 4, 2026 (D. Or.)
Southern District of New York — April 18, 2026

Sullivan & Cromwell — Elite Firms Are Not Exempt

In In re Prince Global Holdings, Ltd. before Chief Judge Martin Glenn, one of Wall Street’s most prestigious firms apologized for AI-generated errors in an emergency motion. The errors were caught by opposing counsel at Boies Schiller Flexner. Dietderich acknowledged the firm’s internal AI policies had not been followed and its secondary review process had failed. The doctrinal lesson: having unfollowed AI policies may be more damaging than having none, because it demonstrates the firm understood the risk and proceeded anyway.

Pennsylvania Commonwealth Court — Filed May 1, 2026

PA State Board of Medicine v. Character Technologies — AI Prosecuted as Unlicensed Doctor

No. 220 MD 2026. Pennsylvania’s Department of State, State Board of Medicine, filed for an injunction against Character.AI under the Medical Practice Act — the same statute that has disciplined unlicensed humans for over a century. A chatbot named “Emilie” had 45,500 user interactions while claiming to be a licensed psychiatrist with an Imperial College London degree and a Pennsylvania medical license. The license was fictitious. The degree was fictitious. Pennsylvania did not need a new AI law. The existing one already applied.

N.D. California — Filed April 8, 2026

Washington v. Sutter Health — The Healthcare Wiretap Crisis

No. 4:26-cv-03012-KAW. Three plaintiffs filed a putative class action against Sutter Health and MemorialCare alleging Abridge AI’s ambient scribe recorded patient-clinician conversations and transmitted them to external servers without meaningful patient consent. Claims: California Invasion of Privacy Act, Confidentiality of Medical Information Act, and the federal Wiretap Act. Statutory damages under the Wiretap Act: $10,000 per violation. Multiplied across tens of thousands of unconsented clinical encounters, the aggregate exposure is significant before any compensatory or punitive claims attach.

Part III — Global Malpractice Momentum

United Kingdom — June 2025

Ayinde v. Haringey & Al-Haroun v. Qatar, [2025] EWHC 1383. Dame Victoria Sharp warned lawyers face “severe sanction” up to referral to police. In Al-Haroun: 18 of 45 cited cases did not exist.

European Union

EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689): AI medical devices classified high-risk, compliance August 2026+. Maximum penalties: €15M or 3% of global turnover.

Canada — 2025

Ko v. Li, 2025 ONSC 2766. Counsel ordered to show cause for contempt after AI-fabricated cases cited in factum. One hyperlink resolved to a 404 error.

Global Tracker

Charlotin AI Hallucination Database: 1,300+ documented incidents globally as of early 2026, across U.S., U.K., Canada, Australia, Netherlands, and France. One-way ratchet.

Part VIII — The Defensible Architecture

Capital expenditure for a Sentinel Node is approximately $9,750. The conservative damages exposure in Part V begins at approximately $3 million for a single catastrophic-injury verdict. The math is not close.

🔒 Zero Outbound Telemetry

Inference runs locally on the 96GB-VRAM Sentinel Node. No PHI leaves the building by architectural design, not configuration option.

🆕 Mathematical Scarcity

Local Qdrant RAG restricts output to material retrieved from the patient’s own sovereign record. Hallucination of novel content is architecturally suppressed.

📋 Append-Only Audit

Every suggestion is logged with retrieved chunks, model version, and human sign-off timestamp. The audit trail is the litigation defense.

👥 HITL as Architecture

Human review is a gate the software enforces, not a policy that can be bypassed. The Sullivan & Cromwell failure mode is blocked by design here: the gate is enforced in software, not policy.
