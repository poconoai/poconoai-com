Coexistence · Not Replacement

Works with your clinic's systems.

Independent practices don't worry about efficiency. They worry about disruption. The EHR that took eighteen months to configure for your billing workflows, the fax line your referring physicians use, the shared drive where your staff stores scanned records going back to 2012 — these are the things a practice cannot afford to break. Sentinel Node is designed to coexist with them, not replace them.

The premise: Sentinel Node reads from the documents your clinic already keeps, in the places your clinic already keeps them. It does not require you to switch EHRs, migrate patient records to a new platform, reconfigure your fax workflow, or renegotiate vendor contracts. Your systems stay. The retrieval and organization layer is added alongside them.

The systems Sentinel Node coexists with.

What this looks like for the platforms most independent practices actually run.

EPC

Epic

Coexists · No Integration Required

Epic is the institutional EHR. Sentinel Node does not attempt to integrate with Epic's API, does not sync to Epic's database, and does not require FHIR approval. Instead, Sentinel Node reads from exported documents, printed PDFs, and scanned records your staff has already filed locally — the information that lives outside of Epic's UI but represents the real clinical picture.

What you keep doing: chart in Epic exactly as you do today.

ATH

Athenahealth

Coexists · No Integration Required

Athenahealth manages billing, scheduling, and clinical documentation for many independent practices. Sentinel Node sits alongside it — reading from locally stored exports, scanned records, and documents your staff downloads from Athenahealth to local drives during their normal workflow. No Athenahealth configuration changes required.

What you keep doing: use Athenahealth for everything it does today.

CRN

Cerner / Oracle Health

Coexists · No Integration Required

Cerner deployments in community and independent practice settings typically involve locally accessible document stores and shared network locations alongside the EHR. Sentinel Node indexes those locally accessible documents — visit notes exported to PDF, scanned consult letters, local policy documents — without connecting to Cerner's core system.

What you keep doing: chart and document in Cerner as you always have.

eCW

eClinicalWorks

Coexists · No Integration Required

eClinicalWorks is the EHR of choice for many independent primary care and specialty practices. Sentinel Node coexists with it by reading the documents your staff has saved locally — referral letters, prior auth correspondence, exported summaries, and scanned records — which often contain the clinical context that matters most when answering a retrieval question.

What you keep doing: use eClinicalWorks for scheduling, documentation, and billing.

FAX

Faxed Records & Scanned Referrals

Reads · Indexes

Fax remains the primary document exchange format for most independent practices. Scanned faxes — consult notes from specialists, hospital discharge summaries, prior auth responses, lab results from outside labs — are ingested locally by Sentinel Node through OCR and structure detection. Staff confirm routing before anything is filed; the documents stay in your local scan folder.

What you keep doing: receive and scan faxes as you do today. The retrieval layer is added underneath.

PDF

Local Drives & Document Repositories

Reads · Indexes

Most independent practices have years of clinical documents stored on shared network drives — patient records, clinical protocols, payer correspondence, policy documents, training materials. Sentinel Node indexes these in place without requiring you to move, rename, or restructure them. The folder structure your staff knows stays exactly as it is.

What you keep doing: save documents where you save them today.

OL

Outlook & Email Correspondence

Reads · Selectively

Payer correspondence, referral confirmations, and prior auth responses increasingly arrive by email. With appropriate configuration and staff confirmation, Sentinel Node can index locally archived email exports — surfacing a prior auth approval or a payer policy change that's buried in an inbox from six months ago. This is opt-in and staff-confirmed, not passive email surveillance.

What you keep doing: use Outlook as you do today. Indexing only applies to what you explicitly configure.

BIL

Billing Systems & PM Software

Coexists · Untouched

Practice management and billing systems — whether integrated with your EHR or standalone — are not touched by Sentinel Node. Billing workflows, claim submission, payment posting, and payer portal access remain entirely in your existing systems. Sentinel Node's value to billing staff comes from faster document retrieval for denial review, not from integrating with the billing system itself.

What you keep doing: bill and manage claims in your existing PM system.

What we're honest about.

Sentinel Node is not an EHR integration. It does not connect to Epic's or Athenahealth's API. It does not sync bi-directionally with your clinical database. It does not pull real-time patient data from your EHR as appointments are added or orders are placed.

What it does: read the documents your staff has already exported, printed, scanned, or filed locally — and make those documents retrievable in seconds rather than minutes. The integration ceiling is the local document layer. That layer is where most of the retrieval burden actually lives.

If your operational question is "why can't I find that consult note from six months ago" or "where is that prior auth denial letter" or "what did we document last time for this type of appeal" — Sentinel Node is designed for exactly that problem. If your question is "can Sentinel Node write directly into Epic" — the honest answer is no, and any system that claims to do that for an independent practice without FHIR approval is telling you something worth investigating.

Adoption that starts where you are.

Three phases — each independent of what you do in your EHR.

PHASE 01

Read-Only Retrieval

Sentinel Node indexes your existing local document store — scanned records, fax files, exported PDFs — and makes them searchable. Staff query; staff confirm. Nothing enters or modifies any existing record.

Weeks 1–3 of pilot deployment.

PHASE 02

Workflow Augmentation

Staff begin using retrieval to accelerate specific high-burden tasks: prior auth evidence packages, referral clinical packets, denial review document assembly. Each output is reviewed before it's used.

Weeks 4–8 of pilot deployment.

PHASE 03

Governed Steady State

Audit chain active, governance logs reviewed monthly, workflows calibrated to the specific retrieval patterns that matter most for your practice. Physician oversight built in from the first day forward.

Post-pilot steady state operation.

What stays exactly as it is.

Six operational commitments about your existing environment.

  • Your EHR workflow stays intact. Physicians chart in the same system, the same way. Nothing about Sentinel Node changes the clinical documentation workflow inside your EHR.
  • Your fax line stays operational. Sentinel Node reads scanned faxes; it does not replace the fax workflow or require any fax system changes.
  • Your billing system is not touched. Claim submission, payment posting, and payer portal access remain in your existing system. Sentinel Node does not connect to it.
  • Your staff's folder structure stays as it is. Documents are indexed in place. Files do not need to be moved, renamed, or reorganized for the system to read them.
  • Vendor contracts are not disrupted. No EHR renegotiation, no FHIR approval process, no new vendor relationships required to deploy Sentinel Node.
  • Patient records do not leave the building. Sentinel Node processes documents on the appliance located in your office. PHI does not traverse a public network for retrieval, inference, or audit logging.
