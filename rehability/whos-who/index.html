👤 Who's Who

A planned personal context helper for people who have difficulty recognizing people or faces. The goal is not surveillance. The goal is dignity — helping a user privately remember who someone is, how they know them, and what context matters before a conversation.

Share your input → All Rehability tools
Why This Tool Exists

When faces don't stick, context does.

Some people have difficulty recognizing faces or reliably linking a face to a name, role, or relationship. This is sometimes called prosopagnosia (face blindness) but it describes a broader range of experiences — people who struggle to place faces in context, who lose the connection between a face and a name under stress or fatigue, or who simply have more difficulty with recognition than most people assume is normal.

"When someone walks toward me and expects me to remember them, my mind goes blank. Not because I don't know them — I may know them well. But I can't connect the face I'm seeing to the person I know. I need a moment of context to get there."

This is not a trivial social awkwardness. It affects professional relationships, family interactions, medical appointments, and everyday dignity. Calling someone by the wrong name, failing to recognize a colleague, or appearing indifferent to someone who matters to you — these carry real social cost.

Who's Who is designed to help with that moment before the conversation. Privately. Locally. Under your control.

What It Would Help With

That moment before you have to speak.

These are the kinds of situations Who's Who is designed to support. None of these scenarios involve surveillance. All of them are about the user accessing their own notes about their own relationships.

🏘️
Neighborhood
"The person walking toward me on the block — who are they?"
Tool surfaces: your note from a year ago — "Neighbor from the HOA meeting. Lives at the corner. Their name is Carol. Friendly — we talked about the sidewalk repair." Context restored before you reach them.
👨‍👩‍👧
Family / Social
"That's my cousin's spouse, but I can never remember their name or how we know each other."
Tool surfaces: "David. Married your cousin Emma in 2021 at the Poconos venue. Works in construction. You talked about the Eagles game at the reunion last summer."
🏥
Healthcare
"The nurse who just walked in — I've met her before but I can't remember her name or what we discussed."
Tool surfaces: "Nurse practitioner. You saw her last Tuesday for the blood draw. She was the one who mentioned the follow-up timing. Friendly tone."
💼
Professional
"I know this person is a business contact but I can't place them before they reach me."
Tool surfaces: "James — investor call in April. COO background. Interested in the medical side of the product. Last note: he wanted to reconnect after the pilot."
🗓️
Before any conversation
"Before I speak, remind me what we last discussed."
Tool surfaces the last notes you made about this person — topics, context, what you promised, what they mentioned — so you can enter the conversation prepared rather than improvising.
Design Boundaries — Please Read

What this tool is. What it is not.

These boundaries are not fine print. They are design requirements. Who's Who will not be built without them, and they will be enforced technically — not just stated as policy.

✓ What Who's Who is

  • A personal memory tool. You add people to your own library. You write the context. You control who is included and what is recorded. It is your notebook, structured and searchable.
  • Local-first. Your people library stays on your device. It does not sync to a cloud server. It does not share your relationship notes with anyone.
  • User-controlled. You decide who is in the library. You decide what context to record. You can delete any entry at any time. Nothing is added without your explicit action.
  • Consent-aware. The design assumes the people in your library are people you already know — people you've chosen to remember context about. There is no mechanism for adding strangers or people who have not been introduced to you.
  • Private by default. Your notes about your relationships are private to you. Caregiver access, if ever added, is a separate opt-in with explicit controls — not a default.
  • An assistive tool for disability support. Designed for people with face recognition difficulty, cognitive load challenges, memory conditions, or social anxiety around not recognizing people they know.

✗ What Who's Who is not — and will never be

  • Not surveillance. This tool is not designed to identify, monitor, or track people without their knowledge. It is a personal memory aid, not a monitoring system.
  • Not a law enforcement tool. Who's Who has no function in law enforcement, security, intelligence, or public safety applications. It will not be marketed or licensed for those purposes.
  • Not a background check or identity verification tool. It does not pull public records, social media data, or third-party databases. It only works with information you have personally entered.
  • Not for covert identification. You cannot point a camera at a stranger and have this tool identify them. It is not designed for that use case and any such feature would violate the design contract.
  • Not a replacement for clinical evaluation. If you believe you have a condition that affects face recognition or memory, professional evaluation is important. This tool does not diagnose conditions, describe medical status, or substitute for clinical care.
  • Not automatic. Nothing happens without your action. The tool does not passively observe, passively identify, or alert on its own. It is reactive to your explicit request.
  • Not an account of other people's private information. Your notes about a person are your notes — your impressions, your context. The tool does not pull or store other people's private data.
How It Would Be Built

Privacy-first design, technically enforced.

The intent is for each design principle below to be enforced in the code — not just described in the documentation. If it can't be enforced, it's not a feature.

Principle 1

Explicit entry only

People enter your library because you added them, manually, one at a time. No automatic population from contacts, social media, or any external source.

Principle 2

Local storage only

Your people library is stored locally. It does not leave your device in a network call unless you explicitly export it. No background sync to any server.

Principle 3

Context only, not identity claims

The tool stores your notes about someone. It does not claim to verify who someone is, confirm their identity, or produce legally binding identification statements.

Principle 4

Delete is real

When you delete an entry, it is gone. No soft deletion, no retention, no "we keep it for safety." Deletion is deletion.

Principle 5

User audit trail

You can see a log of what was added to your library, when, and from what action. You are in control and can verify what the system has stored.

Principle 6

No external lookups

The tool does not reach out to public databases, image recognition APIs, social media platforms, or any network resource during a lookup. All data is what you put in.

Who This Is For

Designed for people who need it, not everyone.

Who's Who is an assistive tool. It is specifically designed for people who have a genuine need for context support in recognizing or recalling the people in their lives.

People with face recognition difficulty

Including people who experience what is often called prosopagnosia — difficulty connecting a face to a name, role, or relationship — even with people they know well.

People with memory conditions

People living with conditions that affect recall of social context — including some forms of cognitive impairment, acquired brain injury, or neurological conditions that affect social memory specifically.

People with high cognitive load

People whose cognitive capacity is reduced by chronic pain, fatigue, medication effects, or other conditions — where social context recall competes with everything else the brain is managing.

People with social anxiety around recognition

People who experience significant distress at the possibility of not recognizing someone and the social consequences that follow — and who would benefit from private, low-pressure context access.

Caregivers supporting someone with these needs

A caregiver or family member who helps someone build and maintain their context library — with the person's explicit participation and ongoing consent.

Anyone who finds social context genuinely hard

The difficulty doesn't need a formal diagnosis to be real. If maintaining social context across relationships is a significant challenge for you, this tool is designed with you in mind.

Current Status

Where this is right now.

⚠ Planned — not yet built

Who's Who does not yet exist as a working tool. This page describes the design intent and the constraints that will guide its development.

The first working Rehability plugin — Marcus's Memory Helper — is available now. Who's Who is the next planned plugin after the Memory Helper stabilizes and the plugin runtime matures.

Development will not begin until the design is reviewed with people who actually live with face recognition difficulty or related conditions. Input from the community is part of the process, not an afterthought.

What "planned" means here

Planned means the design intent is committed and the boundary constraints are established. It does not mean a build date is set, a feature list is locked, or a release is promised. It means this tool is next in the Rehability plugin roadmap, and the design work — including the privacy constraints on this page — is the foundation for what gets built.

No production-grade facial recognition today

The current Sentinel Node / Rehability runtime does not include production-grade facial recognition. Any image-based feature in Who's Who would require careful design review, community input, and explicit user control before implementation. We are not starting from a facial recognition assumption. We are starting from the question: what does someone with face recognition difficulty actually need in that moment?

Help Shape This Tool

We need to hear from you before we build it.

Who's Who will be better — and safer — if the people who would use it are involved in defining what it should and shouldn't do. We're specifically looking for input from:

People with face recognition difficulty

What does the hard moment actually feel like? What context would help most? What would you never want this tool to do?

Disability advocates and researchers

Are there design patterns we should follow or avoid? Are there existing tools or research we should be aware of?

Clinicians working with relevant populations

What would make this tool clinically safe to recommend? What boundaries would give you confidence recommending it to a patient?

Privacy and ethics researchers

Are there constraints we've missed? Are there design choices we should reconsider before building begins?

Tell us what you'd need from this tool.

No sales pitch. No commitment required. Just honest input from people who know what this kind of difficulty actually costs.
