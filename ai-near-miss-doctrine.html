The AI Near-Miss Doctrine

Incident response, root-cause review, and corrective action for regulated AI workflows in healthcare and law.

In regulated work, the most dangerous AI failure is not always the hallucination that reaches a patient, payer, court, or client. It is the near miss that gets ignored, repeated, normalized, and eventually becomes harm.

Why This Paper Matters

Healthcare and legal AI failures are not only model failures. A fluent, confident, polished output can still be wrong — and in regulated work, the cause is frequently somewhere other than the model itself.

Failure is rarely just the model

Errors arise from retrieval failure, stale sources, weak review, unsafe prompts, missing export controls, or the absence of any incident-response process at all.

Polished is not the same as grounded

A reviewer may see a confident paragraph and a plausible citation and assume the system is more grounded than it is. The danger is normalized trust without preserved evidence.

Governance, not generic chat access

Regulated organizations need evidence-preserving governance — custody, retrieval, review, correction, and learning loops — not a chatbot window onto sensitive data.

The Core Doctrine

Responsible AI is not AI that never makes mistakes. Responsible AI is AI that makes mistakes detectable, reviewable, correctable, and defensible before they become harm.

The doctrine is broader than any single product. Sentinel Node is the implementation example, not the doctrine itself. The goal is to create operational evidence whenever AI is used in regulated workflows: evidence of data custody, source retrieval, drafting, review, correction, approval, export, and post-event learning.

The Level 0–5 AI Incident Classification Model

Six levels let an organization distinguish routine blocked outputs from serious incidents while preserving enough signal to spot patterns over time.

Level 0 · Blocked Unsupported Draft

A draft or claim is blocked or flagged before use. Evidence that the governance layer worked — log automatically; escalate only if it repeats.

Level 1 · Reviewer Correction

A human reviewer catches and corrects a weak or incomplete output before external use. Capture as a reviewer note; track for recurring patterns.

Level 2 · Near Miss

The output could have materially affected a patient, payer, client, court, or matter if it had not been caught. Requires formal near-miss review.

Level 3 · Operational Incident

The event reveals a systemic weakness in retrieval, corpus hygiene, access, review, or workflow design. Requires root-cause review and corrective action.

Level 4 · Externalized Incident

A flawed AI-assisted output reaches a payer, court, client, patient, opposing counsel, regulator, or other external party. Escalate to leadership, compliance, or counsel.

Level 5 · Reportable / High-Severity

Potential patient or client harm, privacy breach, malpractice exposure, sanctions risk, or a regulatory reporting event. Formal incident response under governing procedures.

The AI Incident Lifecycle

A log records events. A lifecycle turns events into learning, correction, and evidence. The purpose is not blame — it is evidence-preserving learning.

Detect

Unsupported claims, reviewer corrections, source gaps, export attempts.

Preserve

Prompts, sources, outputs, reviewers, timestamps, decisions.

Classify

Sort the event by severity, Level 0 through Level 5.

Review

The appropriate clinical, legal, or compliance authority examines it.

Correct

Fix the corpus, workflow, reviewer process, or policy.

Learn

Identify recurring patterns before they externalize.

Attest

Show evidence that governance actually occurred.

Why This Matters to Buyers and Investors

For buyers

A demo answers whether a system can draft or summarize. Governance answers what happens when it is wrong. The most important question is the simplest: What happens when the AI is wrong? A vendor that cannot answer with a concrete incident-response model is not ready for regulated deployment.

For investors

As AI becomes easier to build, defensibility moves toward custody, workflow, auditability, and defensibility. The winners in healthcare and law will not promise error-free AI — they will make AI errors detectable, reviewable, correctable, and defensible.
