Plain-Language Briefing for RNs
What Nurses Need to Know About AI in the Chart

A plain-language summary of “The Paradox of Automated Abandonment” — May 2026

Why this matters to you
  • AI is now writing parts of the chart — drafts, summaries, and care plans.
  • If you sign it, it’s your note. Your license is on the line, not the computer’s.
  • Wrong AI notes can risk patient safety and trigger chart audits or board review.
What actually happened (April–May 2026)
1
Oregon: Lawyers were fined $110,000 after filing court papers with fake AI-made citations.
2
New York: A large law firm had to publicly apologize after AI errors showed up in filings.
3
Pennsylvania: The state sued Character.AI after chatbots posed as real doctors and gave medical advice.

Same pattern everywhere: AI made it up, a person signed it, and the person took the blame.

What this means for nurses

If AI writes it and you sign it, you may be responsible. The chart shows your name, not the AI’s. Courts and boards are treating AI like a spell-checker — helpful, but not accountable.

Hospitals also cannot hide behind “the AI did it.” Leaders are being told they must check AI tools before use, watch them in real time, and fix problems fast. “We didn’t know” is no longer a defense.

Three questions to ask at work
  1. 1
    Who reviews AI notes before I sign? Is there a clear, real-time check?
  2. 2
    Where is the AI data stored? Is patient data leaving the hospital system?
  3. 3
    Can I see what the AI used? Can I view the source or reasoning behind a suggestion?
Bottom line

You are still the licensed professional. AI is a tool, not a shield. Read every AI draft like you wrote it yourself, fix errors, and only sign what is true and safe for your patient.
