On-Premises Legal AI · Pocono AI

Private AI for Law Firms.
No client data leaves your building.

The Sentinel Node is on-premises legal AI designed for independent and small law firms that cannot afford to route privileged client documents through public AI servers. It retrieves from your local files, drafts with citations, and requires attorney review before any output moves forward. Legal AI without cloud exposure — by architecture, not by policy.

Why law firms need private AI

Mainstream AI assistants route your queries and documents to external servers. For law firms, that creates real risk: data leaving the building, potential privilege considerations, and client confidentiality exposure that your professional responsibility obligations don’t accommodate.

Private AI for law firms means the model runs on hardware inside your office. Client documents are processed locally. Nothing is transmitted to an external AI provider by default. Your client data stays in your custody.

Zero cloud routing by default

Queries and documents are processed on-premises. No external AI server sees your client files by default.

Attorney review enforced

Every AI-generated output requires named attorney sign-off before it proceeds. Human judgment stays in the chain.

Reviewable AI audit trail (tamper-evident: Stage 2)

Every query, source citation, and review action is logged to a reviewable audit trail for 90-day on-device retention; SHA-256 hash-chaining is the Stage 2 audit-layer.

Source-cited outputs

Every AI draft cites the specific document sections it drew from. No unsupported hallucinations proceed past the gate.

What on-premises legal AI supports

  • Legal document review — Sentinel Node retrieves from your local matter files and drafts summaries with citations. Attorney reviews before any draft is used.
  • Discovery assistance — Identifies relevant documents within your local corpus. No documents transmitted to external servers.
  • Matter intake and research drafting — Drafts initial research summaries from approved local sources. Review-gated before distribution.
  • Paralegal workflow automation — Routine document preparation tasks assisted by local AI with attorney oversight maintained.
  • Client-confidential AI — Because the model is local, client data does not pass through third-party AI infrastructure.

Architecture designed to support confidential legal workflows

Sentinel Node keeps privileged documents local by design. The AI model runs on a physical appliance deployed in your office. Retrieval is performed against your local document corpus. The architecture removes the transmission vector that cloud AI creates.

This design is not a legal guarantee. Privilege determinations depend on jurisdiction, facts, and attorney judgment. Each firm remains responsible for its own professional responsibility analysis. What Sentinel Node provides is an infrastructure posture that does not create cloud exposure of privileged materials by default.

Important: Pocono AI does not guarantee attorney-client privilege protection, does not provide legal advice, and does not certify compliance with ABA Model Rule 1.6 or any state-equivalent rules. Each attorney and firm must evaluate their own professional obligations with qualified counsel. Sentinel Node is an infrastructure tool designed to keep documents local — it is not a substitute for attorney professional judgment.

Compared to cloud AI tools

Cloud AI (ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.)

Client documents transmitted to external servers. No control over data retention. No reviewable audit log. No enforced attorney review. Privilege exposure risk.

Sentinel Node (local AI)

Zero cloud routing by default. Documents stay in your building. Reviewable audit trail. Attorney review enforced before outputs proceed. Evidence-grounded AI.

Who this is designed for

Sentinel Node is built for independent law firms, solo practitioners, and small-firm practices of 1–25 attorneys who:

  • Handle sensitive or privileged client matters that cannot be routed through public AI
  • Need AI assistance for document review, research, or drafting without cloud exposure
  • Require a reviewable audit record of AI-assisted work
  • Want local AI infrastructure they control, not SaaS they depend on
  • Are evaluating AI adoption under ABA Model Rule 1.6 or state professional responsibility rules
