Who the pilot is for

Independent medical practices (1–10 clinicians) and small law firms (1–15 attorneys) in the Pocono Mountains, Philadelphia, New York, New Jersey, and surrounding Northeast region — close enough for the founder to be on-site.

You’re a fit if…

Documentation, prior authorization, discovery, or records chaos is costing real hours; your data can’t go to a cloud AI vendor; and you can name one person willing to review drafts for sixty days.

You’re not a fit if…

You want autonomous AI, cloud convenience, or a tool nobody has to supervise. Sentinel Node is built around review — that’s the point, not a limitation we apologize for.

What your practice or firm receives

✓

A Sentinel Node appliance installed inside your network — dedicated hardware, deployed and maintained by Pocono AI.

✓

One configured workflow (menu below), indexed only against document sources you explicitly approve.

✓

A review interface where your named reviewer approves, edits, rejects, or escalates every draft.

✓

An append-only, reviewable audit log of every query, retrieval, draft, and decision — yours, on your hardware.

✓

Direct founder support for the duration of the pilot, and a written 60-day evidence report at the end.

Workflows in scope

Choose one to start. Each is a drafting-and-retrieval workflow over your own documents. None involves autonomous action.

Medical

Prior authorization packet drafting · denial & appeal letter drafting · pre-visit chart summaries · inbox/document triage support for clinical staff.

Legal

Privileged local document review support · discovery intake & organization · deposition prep packets · matter chronology assembly.

What data stays local

All of it, by default. Your documents are indexed and processed on the appliance inside your network. There is no routine routing of patient or client files to any cloud AI service, and the default network posture is verifiable at your own firewall. The only routine inbound traffic is signed software updates; nothing leaves the building without an explicit, logged decision by your practice.

What human review means here

Review is a structural gate, not a policy. Every output is a draft until your named, qualified reviewer — physician, attorney, or designated staff under their supervision — signs off in the review interface. The system cannot file, send, fax, or finalize anything on its own. Reviewer identity and decision are written to the audit log on every item.

The implementation timeline

Weeks 0–1 · Scoping

A conversation, a site/network review, workflow selection, and a written pilot scope you sign before anything ships.

Week 2 · Installation

Appliance installed inside your network; approved sources indexed; reviewer trained (about an hour).

Weeks 3–8 · Supervised use

Your team uses the workflow on real, bounded work. We support, tune, and log. A brief check-in each week.

Week 9 · Evidence review

We deliver a written report: time-on-task before vs. after, drafts produced vs. approved/edited/rejected, audit-trail completeness, and your reviewer’s assessment. Then you decide — continue, expand, or we remove the hardware.

What success is measured by

Three things, agreed in writing before we install. We report the numbers either way.

1 · Measured time

Time on the chosen workflow, before vs. during the pilot.

2 · Reviewer trust

The share of drafts useful enough to approve or lightly edit.

3 · Audit completeness

Every output traceable to its sources and its reviewer.

What the pilot does not do

It does not make clinical or legal decisions. It does not connect to your EHR or DMS by API in the pilot phase — documents enter through controlled folder ingestion. It does not train any external model on your data. It does not promise outcomes, savings, or compliance. It does not lock you in: if it isn’t earning its place at day 60, we take the hardware back.

What you provide

One workflow owner and one named reviewer with about 2–3 hours per week; a place to put a small appliance (standard outlet and network drop — we’ll confirm specifics in scoping); the documents for the chosen workflow; and candid feedback. That’s the whole ask.
