Sentinel Node Legal Workflow

E-discovery review without sending production to a cloud.

Modern e-discovery productions can run to hundreds of thousands of documents. The retrieval problem is real; the temptation to send the production to a hosted AI service is also real. Pocono AI’s position is that the production stays on the firm’s premises, and the AI runs against it locally. The associate remains the reviewer.

The e-discovery posture in one sentence

The Sentinel Node is given access to a discovery production as a bounded corpus. The associate queries the corpus in natural language; the system returns retrieved passages with citations to the source documents. No part of the production traverses the public internet. No external service is queried. Every query and every retrieval is logged.

Four operational properties

1. The production stays on premises

Documents are ingested into the Sentinel Node’s local retrieval index. No upload to a hosted vector database. No retrieval API hosted by a third party. The production is at rest on firm-controlled hardware throughout the matter.

2. Role-based access mirrors the review structure

Review teams are partitioned. Associates see only the documents assigned to their review group. Lead counsel sees the union. Conflict walls are enforced at the retrieval layer, not by reminder emails.

3. Every retrieval is logged

The audit log records who queried what, when, which documents the system returned, and whether the user opened the source. Privileged-document retrievals are flagged in the log so a privilege challenge has a clean evidentiary record.

4. The associate is the reviewer of record

The system returns retrieved passages with citations. It does not classify documents as responsive or non-responsive. It does not designate work product or privilege. Those determinations are the attorney’s, logged with attestation.

What this changes for a typical review

An associate reviewing a 60,000-document production typically begins by reading a privilege log and a few sample responsive documents to calibrate the issues. Then a sweep, often with a CAR or TAR tool, often hosted, often with substantial training overhead. The Sentinel Node compresses the calibration step: an associate can ask plain-language questions across the production and receive cited retrievals from the very first day, without the production leaving the firm.

The work that remains attorney work — responsiveness calls, privilege designations, work-product designations, deposition prep mapping — remains attorney work. The work that benefits from machine-assisted retrieval — finding the email chain about the contract amendment, locating every reference to a particular custodian, surfacing documents that mention a specific date range — benefits from machine-assisted retrieval.

What the system will not do

  • It will not produce a responsiveness designation. Responsiveness is a legal judgment.
  • It will not produce a privilege designation. Privilege is a legal judgment.
  • It will not generate a privilege log. The log is built from attorney designations with the audit-log evidence trail.
  • It will not transmit production documents to any external service.
  • It will not summarize a document without citing the source passages used.

Where this fits with the rules

The technical posture is consistent with FRCP Rule 26 obligations around proportionality and confidentiality, and with ABA Model Rule 1.6’s duty to make reasonable efforts to prevent inadvertent disclosure of information relating to the representation. Local retrieval reduces the surface area for inadvertent disclosure to vendors, sub-processors, and incident-response chains. It does not, by itself, resolve obligations the rules impose — it makes meeting them more tractable.
