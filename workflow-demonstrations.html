Sentinel Node Workflow Demonstrations · What Real Use Looks Like

Six workflows. Same architecture. Same audit chain.

The Sentinel Node is not a chatbot. It is a workflow appliance. This page walks through six concrete operational sequences — prior authorization appeals, clinical policy retrieval, citation-bound legal drafting, escalated governance review, audit trail export, and the physician sign-off that the system refuses to skip.

A · Prior Auth Appeal B · Policy Retrieval C · Legal Drafting D · Governance Review E · Audit Trail Export F · Physician Sign-off
All six workflows share the same audit chain — every retrieval cited, every output reviewed
Workflow A · Clinical

Prior authorization appeal, drafted with policy citations

A denial arrives. The practice has 48 hours of effective working time to respond. The clinician opens the case in the Sentinel Node review interface and a draft appeal is prepared against the payer’s own policy — nothing else.

▌ Without Sentinel

Clinician time
60–90 min, often after hours
Source of policy quotes
memory, prior letters, search engines
Citation traceability
manual, partial, inconsistent
Audit trail
scattered across email, EHR notes, and the clinician’s drafts

▌ With Sentinel

Clinician time
8–15 min review & sign
Source of policy quotes
the payer’s ingested medical policy PDF
Citation traceability
every claim linked to a page & section
Audit trail
one sealed record — question, evidence, draft, sign-off

Workflow B · Clinical

Clinical policy retrieval — the policy answer, not a guess

A clinician needs to know what the patient’s payer actually requires for a specific procedure. They ask the node. They get an answer drawn from the payer’s 2025 medical policy — with the page and section visible alongside it.

▌ Cloud chatbot

Answer source

Specific to your payer

Citation

Risk

▌ Sentinel retrieval

Answer source
your ingested payer policy library
Specific to your payer
yes — that payer, that year, that section
Citation
every claim links to page + section + hash
Risk
if the source is not in the library, the model says so

Workflow C · Legal

Citation-bound drafting for legal memos and motions

An attorney needs a section drafted — perhaps a fact-and-procedural-history paragraph, or a summary of the controlling authority. The node drafts from the case file the firm has ingested. Every claim ties back to a Bates-stamped exhibit or a cited case.

▌ General-purpose LLM

Drafting source
training data + open web
Citation quality

Confidentiality

Diligence trail
none — cannot show how the draft was produced

▌ Sentinel drafting

Drafting source
the firm’s ingested case file + cited authorities
Citation quality
every cite verified against the ingested record
Confidentiality
nothing leaves the appliance — privilege intact
Diligence trail
the entire drafting session is replayable from the audit chain

Workflow D · Cross-domain

Governance review — when the system declines to act

Not every case clears the gate. When evidence is ambiguous, conflicting, or missing, the node escalates. It refuses to make the call. It hands the case to a human — and tells them exactly why.

▌ Optimistic AI

On ambiguity
picks one interpretation, presents it as fact
On missing source
fabricates a plausible-sounding citation
On conflict
silently averages or hedges
On low confidence
no signal — output looks the same as a high-confidence answer

▌ Sentinel governance

On ambiguity
surfaces both readings, asks the reviewer to choose
On missing source
declines and logs the gap
On conflict
shows the conflict, holds the draft
On low confidence
marks the draft, requires elevated review

Workflow E · Compliance

Audit trail export — for a regulator, an auditor, or your own counsel

When someone asks how a decision was made — a payer auditor, an OIG investigator, a malpractice carrier, opposing counsel — the practice or firm exports the relevant audit entries from the node. The output is verifiable from the appliance alone.

▌ Cloud AI audit posture

Where logs live
vendor cloud, partial, vendor-controlled retention
Producible on demand
subject to vendor SLAs and discovery process
Tamper evidence
trust the vendor
Reproducibility
limited — depends on what the vendor kept

▌ Sentinel audit posture

Where logs live
on the node, append-only, hash-linked
Producible on demand
exportable from the local appliance — no vendor in the loop
Tamper evidence
any modification breaks the chain mathematically
Reproducibility
the full decision sequence reconstructs from the chain

Workflow F · Sign-off

Physician sign-off — the act the system refuses to skip

Every other workflow on this page terminates in a sign-off. The system is designed so that sign-off is a state, not a setting. Nothing leaves the node, nothing reaches a patient or a payer or a court, until a named human takes a recorded action.

▌ “Human in the loop” as a checkbox

Sign-off mechanism
an optional setting on a vendor dashboard
Reviewer identity
shared logins, generic accounts, often missing
Audit of the action
“approved at 14:08” — that is the whole record
Reversibility
edits made by the reviewer often not captured

▌ Sign-off as a state

Sign-off mechanism
a refused-to-skip step the system enforces
Reviewer identity
named person, role, credential ID
Audit of the action
what they signed, what they edited, when they signed it
Reversibility
every edit produces a diff stored alongside the original draft
